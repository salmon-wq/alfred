### 3. EU Parliament Approves 16-Month Delay on High-Risk AI Act Obligations
**Source**: Morgan Lewis / European Commission | **Category**: Policy

On June 16, the European Parliament gave final approval to material amendments to the EU AI Act, pushing the application date for standalone high-risk AI systems (covering education, employment, credit scoring, law enforcement, and critical infrastructure) from August 2026 to December 2027. The amendments also simplify several regulatory requirements. The delay reflects intense lobbying from industry and acknowledgment that the original timeline was logistically infeasible — but it also gives AI deployers an additional 16 months without binding obligations in high-stakes domains.

### 4. Colorado Effectively Pulls Back Its AI Act Ahead of June 30 Deadline
**Source**: Hunton / Alston & Bird | **Category**: Policy

What was billed as the United States' first comprehensive state AI law effectively died of political attrition this month. Governor Polis signed SB 189 in May 2026, delaying Colorado's original June 30, 2026 effective date to January 1, 2027 and gutting its risk-based framework. The revised law abandons the duty of care against algorithmic discrimination, eliminates deployer impact-assessment requirements, and replaces them with a narrower disclosure regime. Enforcement authority rests solely with the Colorado AG, with no private right of action.
