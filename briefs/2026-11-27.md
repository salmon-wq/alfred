## Top Stories

### 1. Canada Expands AI Compute Access Fund to C$1B
**Source**: Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (canada.ca) | **Category**: Policy

Minister Evan Solomon announced in Toronto on Nov 26 that the fund will receive C$700M on top of its original C$300M. It will pay up to 80% of eligible compute costs bought from "sovereign" providers, meaning companies that are Canadian-incorporated, Canadian-owned and Canadian-controlled and that keep their data in Canada. Other Canadian-based clouds get up to 50%. Projects of C$100K–C$5M qualify through Mar 31, 2030, and a health and life-sciences call has opened. The 30-point gap between the two rates is an industrial-policy lever: it steers public money away from US hyperscalers' Canadian regions.

### 2. Outside Endorsement Builds for AI-Assisted Riemann Proof
**Source**: Scientific American / Science / Cryptonomist | **Category**: Research

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a Lean-formalized proof of the Riemann hypothesis on Nov 20. It was produced with an unreleased Claude model and also covers the generalized hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative said on Nov 21 that it checks against Mathlib's own statement of the conjecture. Scientific American now reports that independent mathematicians consider it almost certainly correct. The caveat is substantial: no human-readable argument has been published, and the AI-generated explanation reportedly runs past 250,000 pages. The result is formally checked but not yet understood by anyone. *Confidence: medium. Search coverage is inconsistent, partly because of an earlier Scientific American piece that debunked an August claim about a related problem. I could not open the primary sources this run.*

### 5. Meta AI Glasses in Amsterdam Court
**Source**: NL Times / DutchNews / MLex / Mixed | **Category**: Policy

The Dutch consumer association Consumentenbond argued its summary case against Meta at the Amsterdam District Court on Nov 26. It wants changes to the Ray-Ban Meta glasses and their default settings, or a ban on Dutch sales. A parallel case from Offlimits and The Firewall cites hundreds of women and girls secretly filmed with the glasses and objects to Meta using footage for AI training. Meta says the recording light, tamper detection and account sanctions are enough. No ruling has been reported. Summary proceedings usually produce a decision within weeks.
