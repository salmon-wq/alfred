## Top Stories

### 1. U.S. extends AI chip licensing to Southeast Asia as Nvidia faces leadership crisis
**Source**: Al Jazeera, The Star, Data Center Knowledge | **Category**: Policy

On Nov 17, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim rule. Exporters of advanced chips now need a license to ship to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, which the rule names as diversion risks, and the rule also targets Chinese developers' remote access to data centres in those countries. Nvidia shares fell more than 4% on the news. The rule follows the scandal around Singapore-based Megaspeed, which the Justice and Commerce departments are now criminally investigating. Several outlets report that Jensen Huang has stepped down as Nvidia CEO, with CFO Colette Kress as interim chief. *We could not confirm the resignation from an Nvidia filing or press release; treat it as reported, not verified.*

### 2. Anthropic's record IPO: priced at $83, opened at $111.50
**Source**: TechCrunch, ITV/AP, TechRepublic | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic priced its IPO on Nov 18 at $83 per share, above its $70–$78 range, for a valuation of about $1.5 trillion. Shares began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on Nov 19 and opened at $111.50. ### 3. ARTEX: an open-source AI pen-testing tool turns into an attack framework
**Source**: Dark Reading, Cybersecurity Dive, CSO Online | **Category**: Research / Safety

Microsoft Threat Intelligence says ARTEX, an agentic penetration-testing framework published on GitHub in July 2026, is now used by Storm-0940, a group Microsoft describes as China-state aligned. In some intrusions that group reached privileged cloud access in under three hours. Since October, Storm-3358, a financially motivated group based in Cambodia, has also used ARTEX against financial institutions, mainly in Southeast Asia (reported Nov 6). Microsoft's CISO has called for ARTEX-class tools to be regulated, and some security practitioners argue that regulation would hamper defenders more than attackers. This is the clearest public case so far of agentic AI used for offense at scale.

### 4. Character.AI bankruptcy sale heads to auction under scrutiny from 40 states
**Source**: Decrypt, PPC Land, NY Attorney General | **Category**: Policy / Industry

Bids for Character.AI's assets were due Nov 24. Disney is the $20M stalking-horse bidder for a license to select assets, and a newly formed LLC has bid $10M. Reported court dates point to an auction on Dec 1 and a sale hearing on Dec 3–4. A coalition of 40 state attorneys general, led by New York, wants any buyer bound by privacy and child-safety conditions, because the assets include users' private chats and mental-health disclosures. The outcome could set a precedent for how chat logs are treated as assets when an AI company fails.

### 5. Inside enterprise AI: an Anthropic field study, and DeepMind agents running Gemini experiments
**Source**: The Neuron (summarising Anthropic and the DeepMind podcast) | **Category**: Research

An Anthropic study of 58 U.S. companies and nearly 300 AI deployments, reported Nov 25, found that 55% of deployments improved output or quality and 19% have delivered no business value so far. The most common blockers were organisational: more AI output than people can review, employee resistance, and mandatory expert sign-off. Separately, Google DeepMind research VP Koray Kavukcuoglu said agents now control part of the lab's compute and run experiments for upcoming Gemini models on their own, though he said full recursive self-improvement is still far off.

### 6. Fight over who regulates AI: governors push back on federal preemption
**Source**: PYMNTS, Townhall/Nextgov, InsideAIPolicy | **Category**: Policy

The National Governors Association's bipartisan AI council wants independent audits of frontier models and mandatory reporting of serious safety incidents. It also opposes broad federal preemption of state AI laws, while a draft federal framework would reportedly preempt state laws for three years. In Congress, Reps. Beyer, Jacobs and Maloy outlined a SAFE AI Act covering safety frameworks and incident reporting for frontier developers. Meanwhile, New York's RAISE Act takes effect in January.
