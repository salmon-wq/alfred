### 2. OpenAI ships GPT-6 Sol and Luna at half the price of GPT-5.6
**Source**: The New Stack / gHacks / Artificial Analysis | **Category**: Models

OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna on 22 September, 19 days after GPT-6 Astra. That completes a three-tier lineup: Astra for the hardest work, Sol for complex coding and professional tasks, and Luna for fast, high-volume work. API prices per million tokens are $2 in / $10 out for Sol (GPT-5.6 Sol was $4 / $20) and $0.10 in / $0.50 out for Luna (GPT-5.6 Luna was $0.20 / $1.20). The API model IDs are `gpt-6-sol` and `gpt-6-luna`. Both are rolling out in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users. Teams still migrating off GPT-5.5 before its 14 October retirement now have a cheaper place to land.

### 3. CENTCOM changes its AI targeting after the Minab school strike
**Source**: Bloomberg / ThePrint | **Category**: Industry

Bloomberg reports that seven months after two Tomahawk missiles hit the Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab, Iran, US Central Command has changed its AI-assisted targeting process. The 28 February strike killed more than 150 people, including at least 123 children. The Pentagon's investigation blamed outdated intelligence, stale satellite imagery and over-reliance on AI-assisted targeting. The school had been separated from a former military site years earlier, but that update never reached the authoritative targeting database. The changes are: new procedures for refreshing and vetting planned targets, open-source data feeds that track civilian movement, dozens of upgrades to Palantir's Maven Smart System (including a pass that re-checks intelligence for anything that should disqualify a target), and AI agents that keep re-evaluating intelligence instead of relying on static snapshots. The fix for a failure partly caused by AI is more AI, which deserves a close look.

### 4. Humans and AI complete all 25,000 cases of an inverse Galois problem
**Source**: Scientific American | **Category**: Research

Professional mathematicians, amateurs and AI-assisted search together found all of the roughly 25,000 cases of an inverse Galois problem that had been open for decades. Each case means finding a polynomial whose roots have a prescribed symmetry group. Rachel Pries of Colorado State University had put the problem to the American Institute of Mathematics, and it was solved within months of that meeting. It was also the subject of the third SAIR competition, which Terence Tao announced in June. The result is a clear example of AI working at scale alongside people. No model did this on its own.

### 5. Alphabet's Intrinsic open-sources its industrial robotics stack
**Source**: SiliconANGLE / The Robot Report | **Category**: Open Source

At ROSCon 2026 in Toronto, Intrinsic announced Intrinsic Core under the Apache 2.0 license. It includes ROS-compatible real-time control that works across hardware (you can swap arms, grippers and sensors without rewriting drivers), collision-free motion planning, grasp planning, pose estimation built on NVIDIA FoundationPose, simulation and calibration services, and Intrinsic-ROS drivers. The code is on Intrinsic's GitHub. Components already used in manufacturing are now free for developers to build on, which lowers the cost of starting a robotics project.

NPR reports that Senate offices can use basic chatbots such as ChatGPT, but lawmakers and staff are barred from the more advanced AI tools at the centre of the regulatory debate. That gap between the regulators and what they regulate is getting wider, and it matters more as the agent-containment incidents from the past fortnight move toward congressional follow-up.

### 7. Go.AI raises $85M for on-premises AI in regulated industries
**Source**: Business Wire / The AI Insider | **Category**: Industry

Go.AI, which renamed itself from Go Abacus earlier this month, closed an $85 million Series A led by Updata Partners, with GFT Ventures and LAUNCH also investing. Total funding is now $90 million.
