## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic and OpenAI Start a Same-Day Price War
**Source**: SiliconANGLE / Simon Willison / 9to5Google | **Category**: Models

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens, down from Opus 5's $5/$25, with cache reads cut 60% and a claimed 40% lower cost to run on typical workloads. About 90 minutes later, OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Sol at $2/$10 and GPT-6 Luna at $0.10/$0.50, roughly half what their GPT-5.6 predecessors cost. Sol is cheaper per token, but early independent testing puts Opus 5.5 ahead, so the competition is now about capability per dollar rather than list price alone.

### 2. Altman and Amodei Brief the UN Security Council on AI Risk
**Source**: CNN / Axios / Security Council Report | **Category**: Policy

France convened a high-level Security Council briefing on AI and international security. Speakers included Sam Altman (in person), Dario Amodei, Hugging Face's Clément Delangue, and Yoshua Bengio. Amodei proposed three things: narrow global agreements (for example, a ban on AI-enabled bioweapons), systems that let countries check each other's commitments, and shared testing standards with an incident-notification system. Frontier-lab leaders now argue for international guardrails at the UN's top security body.

### 3. Trump–Xi Summit Opens With AI Safety Notifications on the Agenda
**Source**: CNN / CNBC / NBC News / TIME | **Category**: Policy

Ahead of today's summit, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng finished talks in New York. The U.S. side proposed a bilateral system for notifying each other of AI incidents that reach national-security level, plus a new U.S.–China AI dialogue. Officials do not expect a breakthrough. The likeliest result is a restatement of the 2024 Lima commitment that humans, not AI, control nuclear weapons, since neither side wants to slow the race.

### 4. Cisco Talos Discloses CLOSEDQUORUM, an Autonomous AI-Driven Implant
**Source**: Cisco Talos / BleepingComputer / Tech Times | **Category**: Research

Talos described CLOSEDQUORUM as the first publicly documented Windows implant that hands command-and-control decisions to AI models. The 16.4MB Go binary asks up to four providers (DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral, Gemini) what to do next and runs whichever action wins a plurality vote, including credential theft and shellcode injection. Talos has not confirmed use in the wild. It released CAIRN, a toolkit for hunting AI-integrated malware, alongside the disclosure.

### 5. Claude Finds a Novel CRISPR-Like Enzyme System
**Source**: Anthropic / Unite.AI / Reuters syndication | **Category**: Research

Anthropic announced a new life-sciences research group and lab, along with its first finding: an uncharacterized "array-associated reverse transcriptase" (ART) system in bacteriophage DNA. Claude flagged it after running 950 agents for 21 hours over 210M tokens of data. The system's function is still unknown, but the few known systems with similar structure can cut, copy, and paste DNA. That makes it a credible example of AI doing hypothesis generation, not just summarising literature.

### 6. OpenAI Extends Daybreak Cyber-Defense Program to Ukraine
**Source**: Quartz / OpenAI | **Category**: Industry

Announced on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, OpenAI is giving Ukraine's Ministry of Digital Transformation access to its Daybreak cyber models to find and fix vulnerabilities in civilian infrastructure. CERT-UA handled nearly 6,000 incidents in 2025. The deal adds to OpenAI's recent expansion of tiered "trusted access" programs for government and security researchers.

### 7. Alibaba Confirms Qwen 4 in Training, Maps a 5–10T Parameter Roadmap
**Source**: LLM Gateway timeline / Digital Applied | **Category**: Models

At its Apsara conference on September 22, Alibaba said Qwen 4 is in training, with Qwen 4.5 and Qwen 5 planned at 5 to 10 trillion parameters. This follows the September 18 release of Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a 1M-context omnimodal model. China's leading open-weight lab is signalling that it plans to stay at frontier scale despite export controls.
