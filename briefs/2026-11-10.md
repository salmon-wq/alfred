# AI News Brief — 2026-11-10

## Overview
Anthropic released new Fable and Mythos models at lower prices and bought gas generators to power its data centers, Meta launched a cheap model for AI agents, and a breach at Fireworks AI showed that AI infrastructure is now a target for attackers.

## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 at lower prices
**Source**: CNBC, TechCrunch, Fortune, Anthropic | **Category**: Models

On November 9, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 to everyone and Mythos 5.2 to vetted organizations in its Cyber and Life Sciences verification programs. Fable 5.2 is priced at $8/$40 per million input/output tokens, down from $10/$50 for Fable 5.1. Anthropic says it runs about 30% faster and its safeguards block about 55% fewer legitimate requests. Fortune notes the pricing comes in below OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra. The launch comes shortly before Anthropic's expected IPO.

### 2. Anthropic takes over a $3.4B gas-generator order to secure 1.7 GW of power
**Source**: Financial Times (via DCD, Tom's Hardware, Bisnow) | **Category**: Industry

According to the FT, Anthropic is taking over Caterpillar generator orders that OnePWR Solutions had placed, giving it about 1.7 GW of gas-fired capacity within roughly two years without waiting in the multi-year queue for new orders. Until now Anthropic has mostly rented its infrastructure, so buying power equipment directly is a change of approach. It also shows that electricity, not chips, is now the main limit on building AI capacity.

### 3. Meta Superintelligence Labs launches Muse Ember for high-volume agent work
**Source**: TechCrunch, The Decoder, MarkTechPost | **Category**: Models

Muse Ember is Meta's smallest and cheapest Muse model so far, priced at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens with a 1M-token context window. It is built for agent tasks like tool calls, routing and data extraction, and is meant to run alongside the larger Muse Wonder model. Its weights are closed and it is available only through Meta's API. Meta's claims about cost-efficiency are disputed: Claude Haiku 5.5 beats it on FrontierCode 2.0 (34.9% vs 27.2%).

### 4. Fireworks AI discloses a second breach that exposed customer data
**Source**: Seeking Alpha, CyberSecurityNews, GBHackers | **Category**: Industry

The Nvidia-backed inference company, which has more than 10,000 customers, says that on October 24 an attacker got in through a separately breached third-party cloud provider. The attacker reached Fireworks development machines and exposed billing records broadly, plus secrets, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. Fireworks says its inference and training products, which keep no customer data by default, were not affected, and Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 is investigating. Fireworks is telling affected customers to rotate any third-party credentials that were exposed.

### 5. Democrats prepare a wave of AI bills for the incoming Congress
**Source**: Reuters, Foley & Lardner | **Category**: Policy

After the midterm results, Democrats are drafting bills that would require independent testing of advanced AI systems, clarify when AI companies are liable for harm, and protect consumers from data-center-driven electricity costs. Foley expects oversight hearings in early 2027 and sees federal preemption of state AI laws as less likely now. Analysts at Morgan Stanley and UBS say last week's AI selloff priced in more regulatory risk than is likely.

### 6. Nvidia reportedly caps Chinese customers at overseas cloud partners
**Source**: Financial Times, Tom's Hardware, Bloomberg | **Category**: Policy

Nvidia has reportedly told its cloud partners, verbally, to keep at least 51% of new contract sales with non-Chinese companies. The aim is to close the route by which firms like Alibaba and ByteDance rent capacity in Singapore and Malaysia. Southeast Asian partners must also enroll in Nvidia's Fleet Intelligence system, which tracks where chips are. Nvidia has not confirmed the details, and partners say the rule will be hard to enforce.

### 7. AI security and infrastructure deals: Palo Alto–Clutch, Dropbox–Tigris
**Source**: The AI Insider, StartUp Beat | **Category**: Industry

Palo Alto Networks is reportedly in talks to buy AI-agent security startup Clutch Security for about $500M, and Dropbox has bought Tigris Data for $350M to build storage infrastructure for agents. Smaller rounds included Lazarus AI ($43M), geniosAI ($19.1M) and Firmly ($15M Series A). Securing agents and building infrastructure for them is where most of the deal money is going this week. These items come from deal trackers and are not yet confirmed by the companies.
