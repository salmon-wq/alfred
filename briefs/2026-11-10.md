### 2. Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 launch at 20% lower list price
**Source**: Anthropic (primary), Computerworld, Windows Report, Finout | **Category**: Models

On Nov 9, Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, one model with two safeguard levels. Fable is generally available. Mythos has fewer cyber and biology restrictions and is limited to vetted security and life-sciences organisations. List pricing is $8 / $40 per million input/output tokens, down from $10 / $50 for 5.1. An optional fast mode generates up to 2.5× faster at double the rate. All benchmarks so far are Anthropic-reported: 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 at xhigh effort (vs 32.4% for Fable 5.1), 74.2% on Humanity's Last Exam with tools (vs 57.2% for GPT-6 Astra), and 76.3% on CyScenarioBench for Mythos. Gemini 4 Carbon still leads AutomationBench, 53.9% to 51.4%. Per token, Fable 5.2 still costs twice as much as Opus 5.5 ($4 / $20). A price cut on the flagship one day before the price range is a clear pitch to IPO investors on margin and demand.

### 3. Nvidia loses close to $200B in a day over the Huang probe
**Source**: Bloomberg, CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Forbes | **Category**: Industry

Nvidia closed Monday, Nov 9, down 4.27% at $178.67, while most other chip stocks rebounded. Forbes puts the loss in market value at about $190B. The board's independent committee is examining Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive whom U.S. intelligence reportedly believes was passing information to Beijing. Nvidia says it has no evidence that national-security or company secrets were compromised. Huang's reply to the three House Republican chairs is reportedly due Nov 11, and their Nov 4 letter reportedly proposed a closed-door briefing on Nov 18. Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said on Nov 9 that Congress "will need a full briefing." The governance question has now cost the AI trade's most important stock real money.

### 4. Nvidia reportedly tells cloud partners to keep China demand below half
**Source**: Financial Times (via Reuters/The Standard, TechRadar, The Next Web) | **Category**: Policy

The FT reports that Nvidia has asked overseas cloud partners to direct at least 51% of their services to non-Chinese customers. The guidance covers new contracts worldwide. It targets a known gap: Chinese AI firms rent Nvidia compute in places like Singapore and Malaysia without moving any hardware across a border. Sources told the FT the guidance is not mandatory and has not been strictly enforced, and it is unclear whether the share is measured by revenue, compute or customer count. A Singapore provider said strict enforcement would hurt its business. The report also says Nvidia has more than halved the list of Asian buyers authorized to purchase its chips. There is no official Nvidia confirmation of the terms. It is notable that this surfaces in the same week as the Megaspeed-linked probe, since Megaspeed is a Singapore cloud buyer.

### 5. OpenAI brings Frontier agents to ADNOC
**Source**: The Next Web, Gulf Business, OilPrice | **Category**: Industry

At ADIPEC on Nov 2, ADNOC signed a multi-year deal with OpenAI and G42. Coverage has continued into this week. ADNOC becomes the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier, OpenAI's agent platform, on UAE-hosted G42 infrastructure. The first use case is oilfield master planning, which ADNOC projects could shrink "from months to hours." That is a projection, not a measured result. The deal also covers cybersecurity through OpenAI's Daybreak Blue programme and workforce training, and no financial terms were disclosed. With this deal, enterprise agent platforms are moving from pilots into national heavy industry.
