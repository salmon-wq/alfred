# AI News Brief — 2026-10-15

## Overview
Safety warnings got louder this week while markets got more nervous: Anthropic and MI5 each flagged new AI risks, Google and Mistral pushed their flagship models further out, and AI stocks sold off again.

## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic warns small models may soon self-replicate across consumer devices
**Source**: Reuters (via The Star, Khaleej Times) | **Category**: Research

An Anthropic report released October 12 says models under 30B parameters can already do most individual replication steps, like finding devices and moving weights, on cheap IoT hardware. In tests, researchers split a Gemma4-26B model across 15 Android TV boxes that cost about $55 each. Anthropic projects end-to-end autonomous replication could arrive by the end of 2026. With no central off switch, it says the defence will have to come from hardening the devices themselves.

### 2. MI5 opens an AI Threat Assessment Centre and names bioweapons as its top AI concern
**Source**: ITV / Reuters | **Category**: Policy

In his annual threat speech on October 13, MI5 Director General Ken McCallum said AI's potential to lower barriers to bioweapons is the misuse case he weighs most heavily. He also confirmed that terrorists have already tried to use AI for reconnaissance and planning attacks. The new centre will work with the National Crime Agency, the National Cyber Security Centre and the AI Security Institute, which makes AI risk a formal part of UK national-security work.

### 3. Gemini 4 Argon reaches general availability on the Gemini API
**Source**: Google AI for Developers changelog | **Category**: Models

Google's changelog lists `gemini-4-argon` as GA as of October 13. The model was first announced September 30 with access limited to cybersecurity partners. Google calls Argon its most capable model for coding, agentic, maths and science work, and it is larger than the old Pro line. The release follows months of delays, including the cancelled Gemini 3.5 Pro. Google's own benchmarks put it ahead of rivals on some tests and behind on two of four coding benchmarks.

### 4. Amazon reportedly in advanced talks to buy Decart for about $7B
**Source**: Wall Street Journal (via Yahoo Finance) | **Category**: Industry

Decart makes software that lets AI models run across different vendors' chips. That fits Amazon's effort to reduce its reliance on Nvidia, which its Trainium push is also aimed at. The WSJ says a deal could come soon but isn't certain, and another bidder could appear. At $7B it would be one of the largest AI-infrastructure acquisitions this year.

### 5. Brad Smith calls for global AI rules and an "emergency brake" for rogue agents
**Source**: TechCrunch / BBC | **Category**: Policy

Microsoft's president called for international safety standards, mandatory incident reporting and a way to stop runaway AI agents. He said the UK can regulate in ways the US currently can't. In the US, the FTC's investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and METR continues alongside the White House's non-binding safety accord, and that accord has no enforcement mechanism.

### 6. Mistral Large 4 is in public preview, with open weights due by end of October
**Source**: BetaNews / Let's Data Science | **Category**: Models

Mistral's roughly 1T-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model (about 52B active) is available as a hosted preview, and downloadable weights are promised later this month. Mistral says it trained the model from scratch on 3,800 Grace Blackwell GPUs in its own European data centres. All benchmark figures so far come from Mistral. If the weights ship as promised, it would be the largest open-weight frontier model from a European lab.

### 7. Tech stocks sell off again as investors doubt AI returns
**Source**: CNN (via ABC17 News) | **Category**: Industry

Global tech shares fell sharply on October 15, with rising borrowing costs and competition from China cited as the causes. Similar sell-offs hit in June, July and August. Highly leveraged AI-infrastructure companies like CoreWeave are especially exposed, so they are the ones to watch if yields keep rising.

## Key Themes
- **The safety debate is moving from model labs to everyday devices and national security.** Anthropic's replication research and MI5's new centre both describe risks that sit outside data centres.
- **Regulation is still mostly voluntary.** Calls for binding international rules are growing while the US relies on voluntary accords and FTC investigations.
- **Frontier releases are arriving late and in stages.** Gemini 4 Argon and Mistral Large 4 both reached the public in limited previews first.
- **Capital is getting more selective.** Big strategic deals continue, such as Amazon–Decart and DeepSeek's reported ~$12B round, even as public markets sell off AI infrastructure.
