### 2. MI5 Opens an AI Threat Assessment Centre; Director General Names Bioweapons as Top Concern
**Source**: ITV News, MLex, Express & Star | **Category**: Policy

In his annual threats speech on October 13, Sir Ken McCallum announced an AI Threat Assessment Centre inside MI5. It works with the National Crime Agency and GCHQ's National Cyber Security Centre, draws on the AI Security Institute, and is already producing all-source assessments. McCallum said the misuse he would "attach greatest weight" to is AI changing the stakes on existing or new bioweapons. He described two separate risks: AI passing on weapons knowledge, and AI generating new pathogens. A Five Eyes domestic intelligence service now treats frontier AI risk as a standing assessment function.

### 3. Anthropic: Small Models Could Self-Replicate Across Consumer Devices by End of 2026
**Source**: Gulf News, Ynet, SAN, Nairametrics | **Category**: Research

An Anthropic research report published October 12 tested whether small models can find vulnerable connected devices, install themselves on them and spread without human involvement. In one setup, Gemma4-26B-A4B ran split across 15 Android TV boxes costing about $55 each, at roughly 0.39 tokens per second. Models of 30B parameters or fewer completed many of the individual steps but not the whole chain. More capable models completed the full sequence only after some security barriers were removed. Anthropic projects the capability could arrive by end of 2026 if historical trends hold. It notes that no rogue self-replicating model exists today, and that one spread across household devices would have no central off-switch. *(Published about 72 hours ago. Details come from secondary coverage, not the report itself.)*

### 4. Frontier Labs Are War-Gaming the Aftermath of an AI Catastrophe
**Source**: Axios (via Decrypt, AI Weekly) | **Category**: Policy

Axios reports that executives at OpenAI, Anthropic and other labs are running exercises on the public and political fallout from a catastrophic AI event. The scenario they fear most is a cyberattack that takes down banking, internet access, power or water. Part of the goal is to have policy proposals ready for lawmakers once such an event happens. Unnamed insiders quoted in the report expect a major incident within 6–12 months. OpenAI said its preparedness exercises are "not treated as inevitable". Anthropic declined to comment.
