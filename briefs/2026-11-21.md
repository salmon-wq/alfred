## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic's record IPO cools after a 34% pop
**Source**: CNBC, Yahoo Finance | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 (above its $70–$78 range), raising about $81.3B — the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX. Shares (Nasdaq: ANT) opened at $111.50 on Nov 19, closed the day at $97.17, then fell 9.5% to $87.90 in early trading Nov 20. Valuation estimates diverge widely ($1.27T–$1.8T depending on share count and price used); the stock's first full week will test whether public investors price frontier labs like the private market did.

### 2. House panel discloses 879,000 AI chips approved for sale to China
**Source**: House Foreign Affairs Committee, The Standard (HK) | **Category**: Policy

Chairman Brian Mast released Commerce Department (BIS) licence records showing roughly 879,000 Nvidia H200 and equivalent AMD chips approved for Chinese buyers between December 2025 and May 2026, out of ~3.4 million requested — including 150,000 for Tencent and 75,000 for Alibaba. The disclosure lands days after Nvidia said on its Nov 18 earnings call that it has stopped shipping licensed H200s to China, and after BIS issued an interim rule on remote access to advanced AI chips and servers.

### 3. Google ships Gemini 4 Neon, its mid-tier reasoning model
**Source**: Google Blog, 9to5Google | **Category**: Models

Gemini 4 Neon, released Nov 18, sits between Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen at $3 / $15 per million input/output tokens with a 1M-token context window. Google reports it third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (score 61) and is gating a separate cyber-focused configuration to vetted defenders via its Fairwind Program — another instance of labs splitting dual-use capability into restricted tiers.

### 4. White House readies AI "national champions" list
**Source**: The Wall Street Journal (via The Next Web) | **Category**: Policy

The administration plans to designate more than two dozen US AI firms — reportedly including Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, OpenAI and Anthropic — as "national champions," with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to unveil the list. Reported benefits include fast-tracked export licences and government backing abroad. Awkwardly, the Pentagon still bars its contractors from using Anthropic models; no official list has yet been published.

### 5. Trump tells APEC the US opposes "globalist" AI regulation
**Source**: Bloomberg, CASRAI | **Category**: Policy

At the APEC summit in Shenzhen on Nov 19, President Trump said the US would block AI regulation that stifles development and rejected multilateral rule-making. The stance contrasts with the post-midterm Congress, where several members are pushing AI accountability, and with state action such as New York's new AI enforcement unit for the RAISE Act (announced Nov 17).

### 6. Nvidia nears $500M+ deal for diffusion-LLM startup Inception
**Source**: The Information (via Newsquawk) | **Category**: Industry

Nvidia is reportedly close to acquiring Inception, maker of the diffusion-based Mercury 2 language model, for more than $500M; Nvidia is already an investor. Inception claims 1,000+ tokens/sec output — 5–10x faster than speed-optimised autoregressive models — so the deal would signal Nvidia betting on non-autoregressive architectures for inference. Neither company has confirmed.

### 7. Ex-Alibaba AI chief's Pragmatik Labs doubles valuation to $5B
**Source**: Bloomberg | **Category**: Industry

Pragmatik Labs, founded by former Alibaba Qwen lead Lin Junyang, closed new funding at a $5B valuation, up from $2B in August, per people familiar with the matter. It is the latest sign that Chinese model talent can command premium valuations despite tightening chip access.
