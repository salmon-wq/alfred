# AI News Brief — 2026-10-16

## Overview
Regulators are tightening their grip on frontier labs: the FTC has served formal demands on OpenAI and Anthropic, Character.ai's bankruptcy shows what legal costs can do, and markets are newly nervous about how AI infrastructure gets financed.

## Top Stories

### 1. FTC Serves 50-Page Civil Investigative Demands on OpenAI and Anthropic
**Source**: Axios (via CBS News, Semafor, The Next Web) | **Category**: Policy

The FTC sent each lab a separate 50-page civil investigative demand, a subpoena-like order. It asks for records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing materials and privacy policies, Axios reported Oct 15. The demands turn a probe first reported Sept 30 (which also covers the evaluator METR) into a formal process that can compel testimony. The FTC has made no allegations yet. The probe also puts legal pressure on the labs that signed last week's voluntary accord with the White House.

### 2. Google Opens Gemini 4 Argon to Developers, Subscribers and Copilot
**Source**: Google / GitHub (via Let's Data Science) | **Category**: Models

On Oct 13 Gemini 4 Argon became broadly available through the Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Until then it had been limited to cybersecurity partners after its Sept 30 debut. Introductory API pricing is $2/$10 per million input/output tokens through Nov 30, then $4/$20. GitHub made it generally available the same day in Copilot Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise.

### 3. Character.ai Files for Chapter 11, with Disney as a $20M Stalking-Horse Bidder
**Source**: CBS News / CNN | **Category**: Industry

Character.ai filed for Chapter 11 and is seeking a court-supervised sale. Its CEO blamed "significant legal, regulatory and operational headwinds," after lawsuits linking its chatbots to teen suicides and suits from Kentucky and Pennsylvania. Disney has bid $20M for a non-exclusive IP license, and the sale is expected to close in late Nov or early Dec. This is the clearest case so far of a consumer AI company being taken down by its legal exposure. It also raises open questions about what happens to millions of users' chat logs.

### 4. Proofpoint Agrees to Buy Varonis for ~$6.3B to Secure AI Agent Access
**Source**: SEC filing (Varonis 8-K) | **Category**: Industry

Thoma Bravo-owned Proofpoint will acquire Varonis in an all-cash deal at $49.75 per share, announced Oct 15. The two companies pitch the deal as data protection and access governance for AI agents. It extends Proofpoint well beyond email security. Data-access security is quickly becoming a major consolidation area as companies give agents broad access to their files.

### 5. Asian and US Tech Stocks Fall on AI Funding Worries
**Source**: CNBC | **Category**: Industry

SK Hynix, Samsung and SoftBank led an Asian tech selloff on Oct 15, and Nvidia, AMD and Dell also fell. Analysts tied the drop to concern about financing AI infrastructure as Treasury yields rise, not to weaker demand for compute. It echoes August's yield-driven chip selloff. Funding costs, not appetite for chips, are becoming the main constraint the market is watching.

### 6. Meta's New Publisher Terms Allow Scraping and Training on Partner Sites
**Source**: AI Weekly (aggregated) | **Category**: Policy

Meta's revised business terms take effect Oct 30. They reportedly let it scrape publishers' websites and train on that content. Expect pushback from publishers and copyright litigators, and the change could become a test case for consent-by-terms-of-service. *Not yet confirmed against Meta's own documentation.*

### 7. Deal Flow: Ramp at $60B, Amazon in Talks for Decart at ~$7B
**Source**: TechCrunch / WSJ | **Category**: Industry

Ramp raised $1.85B at a $60B valuation (up from $44B four months ago), led by Dragoneer, Thrive, Founders Fund and others, on the strength of its AI product push. Separately, the WSJ reports Amazon is in advanced talks to acquire real-time video-model startup Decart for about $7B, though a rival bidder could still emerge. Labelbox also closed a Series E of more than $100M.

## Key Themes
- **The legal environment is hardening**: FTC demands, the Character.ai bankruptcy, a White House liability push and new liability bills in Congress all point toward courts and regulators setting AI rules in practice.
- **Money is getting tighter at the infrastructure layer**: rising yields are rattling AI-linked stocks, even as private valuations (Ramp) and acquisitions (Decart, Varonis) keep growing.
- **Agent security is now a product category**: Proofpoint–Varonis and Gemini 4 Argon's cyber-first rollout both treat controlling what AI can access as something companies will pay for.
- **Fights over training data are moving into contracts**: Meta is trying to secure training rights through terms of service rather than licensing deals.
