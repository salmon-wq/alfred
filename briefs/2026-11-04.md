### 1. Supermicro posts $19.9B quarter, up ~298% year over year, and raises FY27 target to $65–72B
**Source**: SEC Form 8-K (Exhibit 99.1); The Next Web; Stocktwits | **Category**: Industry

For Q1 of fiscal 2027 (quarter ended September 30), reported November 3, Supermicro had net sales of $19.9B, compared with $11.1B the prior quarter and $5.0B a year earlier. Gross margin was 12.7% and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $1.81. The company targets $65–72B for FY27, against $39.1B in FY26. The August guidance had been $14.5–15.5B, and Supermicro credits the gap to demand for AI platforms. The risk to watch: the board is running an independent review of transactions related to export controls. The company says the outcome could affect forecasts and previously reported results.

### 2. CrowdStrike buys Fiddler AI and Dreadnode (~$495M reported) and launches an "AI Kill Switch"
**Source**: CyberScoop / SC World / Decrypt / OODAloop (WSJ for price) | **Category**: Industry

CrowdStrike made the announcement at Fal.Con Europe in Barcelona. Fiddler, which builds tools for monitoring and explaining AI agents, becomes Falcon Observe. Dreadnode adds offensive testing of AI systems. The WSJ puts the prices at about $400M for Fiddler and $95M for Dreadnode; CrowdStrike did not disclose terms. Payment is mainly cash, with some stock that vests over time. The Fiddler deal is expected to close by the end of fiscal Q4 2027 (January 31, 2027). The Kill Switch lets a human stop any running agent. This is the fourth AI-security purchase in about 14 months, after Pangea (Sept 2025) and SGNL (Jan 2026), and the first aimed at agents specifically.

### 4. Google turns on personalized Search recommendations for K-12 students by default
**Source**: Syndicated reports (via WSJ / The Verge); The Hans India | **Category**: Policy

Google's admin notice describes a "Search Personalized Recommendations Control" that personalizes students' results using account activity, including location. It is on by default in schools where Search is already enabled. At least one district responded by turning off Search entirely, and districts are asking for advance review of major AI changes. This is a recurring pattern in education technology: new AI features ship switched on, and administrators must turn them off. *The original Verge report was not retrieved; the claim that Google would "disable Search unless schools opt in to AI" circulating on aggregators goes further than the sourced reporting.*
