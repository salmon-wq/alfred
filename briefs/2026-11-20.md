# AI News Brief — 2026-11-20

## Overview
Anthropic's record $81.3B IPO dominates the day, while Nvidia pulls back from China and Washington tightens AI chip export controls.

## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic Debuts on Nasdaq in the Largest IPO on Record
**Source**: CNBC | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic (ANT) priced at $83 a share, above its planned range, and raised $81.3B. That makes it the largest IPO ever recorded. Shares opened near $111.50 on Thursday, then fell about 5% after hours to roughly $92.60. Reported market caps range from $1.5T to $1.8T. It is the first public-market test of what a frontier lab is worth.

### 2. Nvidia Stops Shipping US-Approved Hopper Chips to China
**Source**: Bloomberg / Fox Business | **Category**: Industry

On the earnings call, interim CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia is no longer shipping licensed Hopper data-center products to China. Those products brought in $2.4B last quarter. Nvidia's roughly $130B Q4 forecast leaves China out entirely. The company has not said whether the halt comes from US licensing rules, Beijing's import restrictions or its own business decision.

### 3. OpenAI Ships GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol
**Source**: SiliconANGLE / Digital Trends | **Category**: Models

OpenAI released Astra as its new flagship for professional work and Sol as an efficient default. Both are available in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. OpenAI says Astra is the first model it has released externally that reaches the "High" self-improvement threshold of its Preparedness Framework. Early developer reaction has been mixed, and several users say coding did not improve over GPT-6 Astra.

### 4. US Tightens AI Chip Exports to Southeast Asia and Brazil; Congress Releases China License Data
**Source**: House Foreign Affairs Committee | **Category**: Policy

A new rule restricts shipments of advanced AI chips to data centers in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Brazil. Operators that serve China-based firms have 90 days to obtain US licenses. Separately, Chairman Brian Mast published data showing requests to sell 3.4M AI chips to China. Of those, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) approved more than 879K.

### 5. California Releases Next Steps on Mandatory AI Safety Standards
**Source**: Office of Governor Newsom | **Category**: Policy

Governor Newsom announced the next phase of California's AI safety framework, including mandatory safety standards and oversight recommendations developed under his September executive order. Federal lawmakers are still debating preemption, so California remains the most active state in setting the de facto US rules for frontier AI.

### 6. Google Opens Genie 4 World Model to Developers
**Source**: The Neuron | **Category**: Models

Paid developers can now use Google's Genie 4 world model through the Gemini API and AI Studio. Interactive world models are moving from research demos into developer products, with uses in simulation, games and robotics training.

### 7. Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute for $4.5B
**Source**: The Neuron | **Category**: Industry

Palo Alto Networks agreed to buy Applied Compute for $4.5B. It is the latest large purchase of AI capability by a security company. The deal follows reports that an AI-assisted cyberattack disrupted the European Commission, which shows how quickly AI is being used on both sides of security.

### 8. AI Funding Day: Boltz, MegaRobo, Linq Lead ~$837M in Rounds
**Source**: AI Funding Tracker / Tech Startups | **Category**: Industry

On November 19, AI startups raised about $836.8M across 10 rounds. MegaRobo raised roughly $200M, Boltz raised a $110M Series A for AI drug and molecular design, and Linq raised $90M led by a16z for messaging and voice infrastructure for AI agents. Separately, Nvidia is reportedly in advanced talks to acquire Inception, a startup building diffusion-based LLMs, for more than $500M. That deal is unconfirmed.
