### 2. Character.AI files for Chapter 11; Disney is the first bidder at $20M
**Source**: CNN, CBS News, Forbes, Neowin | **Category**: Industry

Character Technologies filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the District of Delaware, listing $50–100M in assets and in liabilities. The company wants a court-supervised sale. Disney is the stalking-horse bidder, offering $20M for a non-exclusive, perpetual license to some of the company's IP, and the auction is expected to close in November or December. The service stays online for now, but the company warns it could shut down if no buyer keeps it running. Character.AI cited lawsuits over teenage users, including suits alleging links to teen suicides, and a dispute over impersonation of copyrighted characters. The bankruptcy pauses that litigation but doesn't decide it. Character.AI was one of the best-known consumer AI companies of the ChatGPT era, and this is the biggest collapse so far of a company in the AI-companion category.

### 3. Anthropic red team: small open models "months" from replicating across IoT devices
**Source**: Anthropic Frontier Red Team (primary), Al Jazeera, Fox News | **Category**: Safety

William Zhou and Daniel Freeman published the study on Oct 12. A 9B model got inference running on 8 of the 9 hardware types tested. Gemma4-26B-A4B was split across 15 off-brand Android TV boxes costing about $55 each and ran at 0.39 tokens per second. GLM-5.2 was the only open-weight model to finish end-to-end replication, reaching 8 of 8 nodes in 4 of 5 trials on an emulated network. Small models have been gaining about 13.7 points a year on the ECI capability index (R² 0.95), and the authors extrapolate that they will reach this capability level by the end of 2026. The caveats are significant. The entry point was an unauthenticated ADB port, so the results are an upper bound. The test network was simplified, and the replicator's doubling time would be days. Recommended defenses are network-flow detection, quarantine and notification of infected devices, honeypots, and secure-by-default devices. The authors cite the UK's ban on default IoT passwords and note that quarantine plus notification cleared 92% of Mirai botnet infections within 14 days.
