# AI News Brief — 2026-11-11

## Overview
Anthropic put a price on the frontier ($70–$78 a share, up to $1.4T), Google answered with a cheaper flagship, and regulators from Canberra to Washington moved to formalise oversight. Meanwhile a supply-chain breach showed how much user data now sits with third-party inference providers.

## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic sets IPO range of $70–$78, implying up to $1.4T valuation
**Source**: CNBC / TechCrunch / Anthropic | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on November 10. It is offering 825 million Class A shares under the Nasdaq ticker "ANT", which would raise about $57.75B–$64.35B. Seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, have agreed to buy $26B of non-voting stock at the IPO price. Pricing is planned for November 18 and trading for around November 19. The range falls well short of the roughly $2T figure reported weeks ago and arrives during a sell-off in AI stocks, so it will be an early test of public-market appetite for frontier labs.

### 2. Google makes Gemini 4 Carbon publicly available, undercutting Claude on API price
**Source**: VentureBeat | **Category**: Models

Google calls Gemini 4 Carbon its strongest model, aimed at long-horizon, multi-step reasoning. It is now available to paid API users and to AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search and Antigravity. Introductory pricing is reported at $2/M input and $10/M output tokens, rising to $4/$20 after November 30. That is well below Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2, launched November 9 at a reported $8/$40, and it opens a price war at the top tier.

### 3. Australia to create an AI safety regulator with mandatory incident reporting
**Source**: Bloomberg / The Conversation | **Category**: Policy

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced a dedicated AI safety regulator that would be able to impose significant penalties. Frontier firms would have to report security incidents to the government and allow independent evaluation of their models. The government is also weighing criminal-law changes for companies that break the rules, which would make Australia one of the first countries to put binding, regulator-enforced duties on frontier developers.

### 4. Fireworks AI breach widens to expose Notion AI chat transcripts
**Source**: The Register | **Category**: Industry

Fireworks AI first said attackers had reached only synthetic test data. It has since confirmed a second dataset containing Notion AI chat transcripts used for Notion's Insights feature. Notion says fewer than 1% of workspaces were affected and its own systems were not breached. It has disabled Insights, stopped serving those models through Fireworks, and told users to rotate any credentials they pasted into Notion AI. The incident shows that inference vendors are now a direct path to customer data.

### 5. Scott Wiener wins Pelosi's former seat, bringing an AI-safety author to Congress
**Source**: MIT Technology Review | **Category**: Policy

Former California state senator Scott Wiener, author of California's frontier AI-risk legislation, won the open congressional seat formerly held by Nancy Pelosi. His arrival gives AI-safety advocates a seasoned legislator in Washington. One tracker reports that no broad federal AI bill has yet reached a committee vote. Separately, a newsletter roundup reports that the DOJ has announced a Superintelligence Task Force and that 16 governments signed a Paris Call for AI Safety and Security; neither report has been independently confirmed.

### 6. OpenAI acquires AI travel startup Odessia and schedules retirement of legacy voice modes
**Source**: Unite.AI / AI Weekly | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI bought Odessia, an agentic travel-planning startup; founder Francis Davidson announced the deal on November 10, and terms were not disclosed. Separately, OpenAI said ChatGPT's Advanced and Standard Voice modes will be retired on December 11, 2026, with users moved to a new Live Voice mode. Both moves fit OpenAI's push from chat toward agents that carry out tasks for users. The company recently closed an $11B round at a $1.25T valuation.

### 7. Tencent launches standalone AI search engine Yuanbao Search
**Source**: KuCoin News (flash) | **Category**: Industry

Tencent launched Yuanbao Search (so.qq.com), an independent AI-powered search engine that competes directly with Baidu and Google. It extends Tencent's Yuanbao assistant into general search and raises competition in China's AI search market. Coverage so far comes from a single outlet; details on the underlying models and rollout are still thin.
