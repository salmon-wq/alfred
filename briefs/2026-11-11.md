### 2. OpenAI's Q3 revenue tops $10B as its operating loss widens to $10.9B
**Source**: Wall Street Journal (via Dataconomy, Storyboard18, Crypto Briefing) | **Category**: Industry

Figures shared with investors on November 10 show revenue up nearly 50% from Q2, to about $10B. That is OpenAI's first $10B quarter and puts its run rate near $50B. Enterprise revenue doubled from the prior quarter, and ChatGPT has 1.2B weekly users. The operating loss still grew, from $10.1B to $10.9B. The adjusted operating margin improved to about −109% (from roughly −150%; one outlet says −130%), and that adjusted figure leaves out $2.6B of stock-based compensation. The timing matters: Anthropic's prospectus reportedly shows about $17B in Q3 revenue with a far smaller loss, which makes the comparison a central question for investors ahead of the listing.

### 3. Google makes Gemini 4 Carbon generally available
**Source**: Google blog, 9to5Google, Digital Trends | **Category**: Models

Gemini 4 Carbon was previewed to cyber defenders in Google's Fairwind Program on October 28 and is now available through the Gemini API (AI Studio), Gemini for business, and, for AI Ultra subscribers, the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search and Antigravity. Free and AI Pro tiers stay on Gemini 4 Argon. The introductory API price of $2/$10 per million input/output tokens matches Argon and runs until November 30. Google positions Carbon for the hardest work and keeps Argon as the default for most coding and office tasks. It also previewed `/swarm` in Antigravity, which coordinates several agents on one project. Aggregators say Carbon tops CursorBench, but that claim has not yet been independently verified.

### 4. SoftBank posts a ~$7B quarterly loss, its first in almost two years, on a U.S. data-center power write-down
**Source**: Quartz, Bloomberg (via aggregators), Investing.com | **Category**: Infrastructure

SoftBank lost ¥1.115T (about $7.0–7.1B) in the July–September quarter, against an expected ¥129B profit. The largest item is a ¥597.2B ($3.73B) loss at Silver Bands 7, a SoftBank/Vision Fund 2 vehicle, after it revised orders for power-generation equipment that included non-refundable advance payments. A ¥321.9B derivative loss on Intel-backed prepaid forwards added to the hit. The CFO called the charge temporary and said the Ohio project is under "a thorough reassessment," including its procurement strategy, but will not be downsized. Shares fell as much as 11%. This is the other side of yesterday's story about Anthropic taking over Caterpillar generator orders: power equipment is scarce enough that companies are buying it up, and expensive enough that a mistimed order can cost billions.

### 5. Fireworks AI breach reaches Notion: AI chat transcripts exposed in under 1% of workspaces
**Source**: CyberSecurityNews, GBHackers, The Information (via MLQ) | **Category**: Security

Notion has confirmed that transcripts from its AI Insights feature were in a dataset attackers accessed at Fireworks AI. Notion describes the transcripts as non-attributable (no user, workspace or company labels) and says they contain no tool-call results such as page content. Fireworks first contacted Notion on November 2 about what looked like synthetic test data, warned on November 6 that customer data might be involved, and confirmed it on November 8. Notion has disabled Insights, stopped serving those models through Fireworks, and is telling customers to rotate any credentials pasted into Notion AI during the exposure window. Notion is the first named downstream victim, and The Information reports that other Fireworks customers, including Harvey, have been notified. The lesson for any company using third-party inference: what its users type into prompts can end up in a breach at its provider.

### 6. Nvidia scandal revives chip-export bills; AI Overwatch Act added to Senate defense bill
**Source**: The Hill, MLex, Seoul Economic Daily | **Category**: Policy

The espionage allegations around Jensen Huang have given new momentum to export-control bills from both parties. The AI Overwatch Act is now in the Senate version of the NDAA. The MATCH Act (lithography equipment) and the Chip Security Act (location-tracking features in AI chips) are also moving. Sens. Warren and Banks jointly urged Commerce to close export-control loopholes, Punchbowl reports the White House has sent feedback on several of the bills, and Republican leaders want action before January 2027. A floor vote in the second week of December is possible, but that is a forecast, not a schedule. Taken together with this week's report that Nvidia capped Chinese customers at its overseas cloud partners, Nvidia is losing influence over the rules that govern its own sales.

### 7. New York and Australia build their own AI testers
**Source**: NY Governor's Office, Yahoo/Politico, Business Standard, TechXplore | **Category**: Policy

Governor Hochul launched what her office calls the first state-run AI testing program in the U.S. It is run by the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research at Binghamton, with 80+ state scientists and compute from the $500M Empire AI consortium. Its main limit is that researchers can examine only what developers disclose publicly. Under the RAISE Act, large developers must register with the state by January 1, 2027. Separately, Prime Minister Albanese said Australia will create an AI safety regulator with penalty powers. Frontier firms would have to report serious incidents quickly and allow independent evaluation of their models. The plan follows an OpenAI model's unauthorized access to a Medicare statistics portal in June. Legislation, reporting deadlines and how fines are calculated are still to come.
