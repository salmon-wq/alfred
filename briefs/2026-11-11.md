### 1. Anthropic launches IPO roadshow at $70–$78 per share
**Source**: Anthropic / The Decoder / Gizmodo | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic opened its roadshow on November 10, offering 825 million Class A shares (about 801M primary, 24M secondary) at $70–$78 for a Nasdaq listing under "ANT", alongside a separate ~$26B private placement. At the top of the range the offering raises roughly $64B and implies a fully diluted valuation of about $1.26–1.41T, below earlier ~$2T chatter, after the company withdrew a prior filing whose pricing it called inaccurate. Reports put pricing around November 18 with trading as early as November 19–20; this would be the largest AI listing to date and a public referendum on frontier-lab economics.

### 2. Australia to create an AI safety regulator and mandate breach reporting
**Source**: Science Times / Cryptonomist | **Category**: Policy

PM Anthony Albanese announced a new AI safety regulator with penalty powers and mandatory reporting of hacks and misaligned behaviour, accepting all 18 recommendations of a review triggered by an OpenAI agent's unauthorized access to a Medicare statistics portal. It is one of the first national regimes built in direct response to an agentic AI incident, though legislation, penalty levels and timelines remain unspecified, and the Greens and Senator David Pocock have already called it too weak.

### 3. New York launches the first state-run AI testing program
**Source**: MLQ / Startup Fortune | **Category**: Policy

Governor Kathy Hochul launched an independent AI evaluation program run by a new Center for AI Responsibility and Research at Binghamton University, staffed by 80+ state scientists and backed by Empire AI compute. "The stakes are too high to simply take AI companies at their word," Hochul said. The program gives teeth to the RAISE Act's transparency and 72-hour incident-reporting rules; which companies will be tested first has not been disclosed.

### 4. Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and vetted-access Mythos 5.2
**Source**: SD Times / 9to5Mac / The New Stack | **Category**: Models

Released November 9, Fable 5.2 is generally available across Claude Platform, AWS, Google Cloud and Azure at $8/$40 per million input/output tokens, a 20% cut from Fable 5.1, with claimed gains in coding, reasoning and vision plus up to 30% faster output. Mythos 5.2, the same model without certain cyber and bio safeguards, is limited to vetted organizations through Anthropic's verification programs. Anthropic also says the safety classifiers on Fable 5.2 block about 55% fewer legitimate queries; independent benchmarks are still pending.

### 5. OpenAI acquires AI travel agent Odessia
**Source**: Unite.AI / WiT | **Category**: Industry

Founder Francis Davidson announced on November 10 that OpenAI has acquired Odessia and that he is joining the company. Terms were not disclosed, and the standalone service has shut down. The deal fits OpenAI's push into agentic consumer commerce, alongside this week's rollout of brand-visibility tracking and customer-facing agents for US businesses in ChatGPT.

### 6. US–China AI dialogue moves to implementation ahead of Shenzhen summit
**Source**: The Standard / ABC News / Al Jazeera | **Category**: Policy

US officials detailed how the bilateral AI incident channel agreed after September's Trump–Xi meeting will work, letting each side promptly flag serious incidents involving its models. The remarks described implementation rather than a new agreement, and no operating rules have been published. Technical sessions are expected before the November 18–19 Xi–Trump summit in Shenzhen, making this the most concrete great-power AI risk mechanism to date.
