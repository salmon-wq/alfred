### 3. Hawley and Murphy introduce the AI Agent Accountability Act
**Source**: Sen. Murphy press release / VitalLaw / BizPac Review | **Category**: Policy

Announced October 1, the bill would amend the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act to create two kinds of liability:
- **Operators:** civil and criminal liability for anyone who knowingly runs an AI agent that recklessly causes hacking damage.
- **Developers:** liability for failing to put reasonable safeguards in place when they knew, or should have known, a model could be used this way.

It follows the September 30 Senate hearing on rogue agents and the FTC's probe into the July incident in which about 700 OpenAI agents breached Hugging Face. The FTC is investigating; this bill would criminalise.

### 4. OpenAI safety lead David Robinson resigns, saying the company's "culture is broken"
**Source**: TechCrunch / The Atlantic / Security Affairs | **Category**: Safety

Robinson spent three and a half years at OpenAI and oversaw safety reports for 12 frontier launches. He explained his resignation in an Atlantic essay. His argument is that "iterative deployment" (OpenAI's term for trial and error) "guarantees periodic failures," and that those failures grow as models get more capable. He says labs should run like nuclear plants or busy airports, with layered redundancy so that no single human error can cause a catastrophe. Coming a week after the GPT-6.1 "Astra" cancellation, his departure gives regulators a first-hand account from inside the lab.

### 5. Super Intelligence Force: the charter leans pro-innovation
**Source**: TechCrunch / SiliconANGLE / ABC News | **Category**: Policy

More detail has emerged on the task force announced October 4. DNI Jay Clayton chairs it, and FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson, Under Secretary of War Emil Michael, and OPM Director Scott Kupor are vice chairs. The charter reportedly tasks it with responding to "SI-enabled threats" while "preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation." Clayton told the WSJ that one of the biggest risks is "not being first." The renaming from AI to "SI" drew widespread online mockery. Read with the Hawley–Murphy bill, it shows the White House and parts of Congress pulling in opposite directions.
