### 2. Great American AI Act Discussion Draft Released
**Source**: House of Representatives | **Category**: Policy

Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a bipartisan discussion draft of the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026 on June 4, representing the first attempt at a comprehensive federal AI governance framework in the United States. The bill covers four areas: Frontier AI Governance, Workforce, Cybersecurity, and Research & International Cooperation. The release of a bipartisan draft is a meaningful signal — the US has operated without federal AI legislation while the EU AI Act and various state laws have moved forward, and Congress appears to be closing that gap deliberately rather than reactively.

**Source**: The White House | **Category**: Policy

On June 2 the White House issued an executive order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," adding a second federal policy instrument to complement the March 2026 National Policy Framework. The EO is focused on accelerating domestic AI development while establishing security requirements for advanced model developers — a dual mandate that reflects the ongoing tension between competitive urgency and safety oversight.

### 4. EU Publishes Code of Practice on AI-Generated Content Labeling
**Source**: European Commission | **Category**: Policy

The European Commission published its Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content on June 10, advancing a key transparency obligation under the EU AI Act. The labeling code is particularly significant for media, advertising, and social platforms operating in the EU, where compliance deadlines are now firm.

### 6. Colorado AI Act Takes Effect June 30
**Source**: Credo AI / Wilson Sonsini | **Category**: Policy

The Colorado AI Act is slated to go into effect on June 30, 2026, eighteen days from today. It will impose significant obligations on AI developers and deployers, including requirements to take reasonable care to prevent algorithmic discrimination. Colorado becomes the first US state to enforce a comprehensive AI law, setting a de facto compliance baseline ahead of any federal legislation — and giving the GAAIA discussion draft additional urgency.
