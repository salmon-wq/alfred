# AI News Brief — 2026-11-15

## Overview
The AI industry enters a decisive week with Nvidia unexpectedly leaderless, Anthropic days from the largest AI IPO on record, and a California lawsuit asking a court to stop frontier models from getting more capable.

## Top Stories

### 1. Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress named interim chief
**Source**: NVIDIA Newsroom / CNBC / Axios | **Category**: Industry

Nvidia announced on November 12 that Huang resigned as president, CEO, and board member effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while keeping her CFO duties, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first independent board chair. The company's 8-K gives no reason. Press reports tie the exit to personal-conduct allegations, which have not been verified. Shares fell sharply, and the board is searching internally and externally for a permanent successor. Nvidia's first earnings report without Huang is due this week.

### 2. Anthropic sets IPO range at $70–$78, valuing it at up to ~$1.4 trillion
**Source**: Yahoo Finance / The Motley Fool / CNBC | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic is offering 825 million shares and plans to price on November 18. Trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker ANT is expected to start November 19. Reports say orders are about six times the shares available. The listing comes in the middle of a broader AI-stock selloff that the Nvidia news has made worse. It will be the first public-market valuation of a frontier lab.

### 3. Nonprofits sue five AI labs to freeze frontier model development
**Source**: TechTimes / Dataconomy (citing Reuters, CBS) | **Category**: Policy

On November 12, the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative, and the Alliance for Secure AI sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and SpaceX's AI unit in San Francisco Superior Court. They ask for an order under California's Unfair Competition Law (Bus. & Prof. Code §17200) blocking models more capable than today's until the companies can show they are under human control. No judge has ruled, and the defendants have not yet answered. The case tests whether state consumer-protection law can be used to restrain AI development itself.

### 4. Google rolls out Gemini 4 Hydrogen to all users
**Source**: Thurrott / Google Gemini release notes | **Category**: Models

Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Google's mass-market Gemini 4 model, is rolling out across the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search for all users. Gemini 4 Carbon, the frontier model, is available to AI Ultra subscribers and on the Gemini API. This completes the Gemini 4 family that began with the limited Argon release on September 30, and it puts Google's newest model in front of its whole consumer user base.

### 5. Post-midterm fallout: Congress flips, data-center limits pass almost everywhere
**Source**: Consortium News / OriginBrief | **Category**: Policy

Democrats won both chambers of Congress on November 3. AI accountability and data-center impacts were campaign issues. Voters in 13 states passed 40 of 41 ballot measures restricting data centers. Progressive Caucus Chair Rep. Greg Casar has called for AI regulation to be a top priority and for AI CEOs to testify. The next Congress, and local permitting, now look less friendly to the industry's buildout plans.

### 6. Three charged over $129M AI voice-cloning CEO impersonation scheme
**Source**: The Next Web / ABA Journal / SecurityWeek | **Category**: Policy

Federal prosecutors in Manhattan indicted Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan, and Guy Sheetrit on wire fraud and aggravated identity theft charges. Prosecutors say the three used AI to mimic executives' and lawyers' voices and images to get accounting staff to send money. Victims include hospital systems in Missouri and California and a Rhode Island bank, and the proceeds were allegedly laundered through crypto. Announced November 5, it is one of the largest deepfake-fraud prosecutions so far.

### 7. Agentic-AI funding stays hot: LangChain raises $300M; Mercor $750M
**Source**: Crunchbase News | **Category**: Industry

Crunchbase's weekly roundup reports that LangChain closed a $300M Series C at a $3B valuation and Mercor raised $750M to connect AI developers with human experts. Separately, Bloomberg reports that Mirendil, founded by former Anthropic researchers, is in talks for up to $1B at a $5B valuation. Private capital is still flowing to agent infrastructure and data, even as public AI stocks fall.
