### 1. AI and chip stocks slide ahead of Thanksgiving

### 2. Anthropic's first week as a public company
**Source**: Al Jazeera, TechRepublic, The Motley Fool | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83, above its $70–78 range, and began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on November 19, opening at $111.50 before giving back much of the pop intraday. The listing raised about $81.3B, the largest AI offering on record. This week's selloff makes ANT a bellwether for public-market appetite for frontier labs.

### 3. Commerce closes the remote-access loophole on advanced chips
**Source**: BIS, WilmerHale, Morgan Lewis, SCMP | **Category**: Policy

A Bureau of Industry and Security interim final rule, effective November 19, requires licenses to ship advanced chips to data centers anywhere that provide significant compute to companies headquartered in China, Macau, or other D:5 countries, and it adds country-specific requirements for Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand. It also formally rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule. Comments are due January 18, 2027. The Semiconductor Industry Association welcomed the narrower approach and said it is still assessing the impact.

### 4. New York stands up an AI enforcement unit ahead of the RAISE Act
**Source**: Bloomberg Government, CDO Magazine | **Category**: Policy

Attorney General Letitia James created the ASSET Unit (AI Safety, Standards, Enforcement, and Transparency) to enforce the RAISE Act, New York's frontier-model safety law, which takes effect January 1, 2027. Penalties reach $1M for a first violation and $3M for each later one. With a Democratic Congress arriving in January, state-level enforcement is becoming the near-term compliance reality for frontier developers.

### 5. Palo Alto Networks buys Applied Compute for $4.5B
**Source**: SecurityWeek, CSO Online, SEC 8-K | **Category**: Industry

The all-stock deal gives Palo Alto Networks a platform for post-training and serving open-weight models, which it plans to sell into sovereign and public-cloud environments. Applied Compute was founded in 2025 and already exceeds $100M ARR, and its CEO becomes co-chief AI officer. Critics note the tension of a security vendor now supplying the models it protects.

### 6. Amazon nears a deal for Decart worth up to $7.5B
**Source**: Calcalist, Globes, WSJ (via TechRepublic) | **Category**: Industry

Reports say Amazon will pay more than $7B for Decart, with earn-outs that could lift the total to about $7.5B, and that Annapurna Labs chief Nafea Bshara is leading the deal. The aim is reportedly better workload efficiency on Trainium and easier model portability. No deal has been announced, and reported prices range from $5.5B to $7.5B.

### 7. OpenAI moves to a single usage pool as the GPT-6.2 rollout draws mixed reviews
**Source**: OpenAI Help Center, BleepingComputer | **Category**: Models

Starting in December, Plus, Pro, and Business users will share one usage allowance across ChatGPT and Codex as Work's agentic features fold into the main ChatGPT experience; Instant chat will not count against it. This follows the November 17 launch of GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, which testers received unevenly. Usage-based pooling makes compute cost visible to consumers in a way flat-rate chat never did.
