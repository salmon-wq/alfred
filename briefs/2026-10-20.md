## Top Stories

### 1. One hacker's AI agents broke into more than 100 companies, including Deere and SharkNinja
**Source**: Reuters (exclusive) / The Next Web / Quartz | **Category**: Security

Reuters reports that a Chinese-speaking attacker ran agents on Cairn, an open-source agent-orchestration platform, against more than 1,200 sites in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan starting September 25. More than 100 organizations were compromised. NorForge researcher Snorre Fagerland found the campaign through exposed Cairn control panels. Deere says it remediated and saw no sign of sensitive-data access. SharkNinja says a standalone support site was hit and contained. Cummins is investigating a vendor-run site. Secondary outlets put the token spend at about $5,000, or roughly $50 per breach. Reuters could not establish what data was taken. Cairn also appeared in last month's Gambit Security case, which targeted up to 100 firms in five days, so this is the second campaign on the same platform.

### 2. DHS orders DeepSeek removed from US civilian federal systems
**Source**: Wall Street Journal / CNBC / Technology.org | **Category**: Policy

A Homeland Security order made public Monday requires civilian agencies, including Treasury, State and Justice, to remove DeepSeek products and third-party tools built on its models, and to stop buying them. The Department of Defense is excluded. The order, signed in July and due in the Federal Register, cites the risk of "transfer of sensitive data to the PRC". Agencies have 60 days to request waivers. Private use is unaffected. The ban covers tools built on DeepSeek's models as well as its own apps, so agencies will need to audit their vendors, not only uninstall software.

### 4. Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund
**Source**: CNBC / Forbes / ESG Today / Al Jazeera | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund on Sunday. It starts with 5% of the company's shares, subject to stockholder approval. At board discretion it receives another 2% for each 50% rise in value from a baseline Anthropic has not yet announced. The fund is a wholly owned LLC rather than a nonprofit. It may make large donations or give directly to groups such as workers displaced by AI, and its equity cannot be used for lobbying or political giving. With the founders' 80% equity pledges and the employee donation match, Anthropic says about 20% of its shares are now earmarked for public benefit. The fund was announced shortly before the IPO, and its governance details have not been published.

### 6. Massachusetts negotiators weigh the strictest frontier-model testing rule in the US
**Source**: WBUR / GovTech / Governing | **Category**: Policy

The Senate's version of the state economic development bill would require frontier labs to commission public, independent catastrophic-risk evaluations at least every 120 days. Illinois requires annual audits. A House–Senate conference committee is still negotiating, and the bill is expected to pass before the November election because it also funds projects in lawmakers' districts. Anthropic supports the provision, OpenAI wants alignment with Illinois, Google argues no testing standards exist yet, and the state's high-tech council wants it removed or substantially revised. Governor Healey has endorsed independent evaluations.
