## Top Stories

### 1. Google Releases Gemma 4 — Open-Weight Models From Raspberry Pi to Server
**Source**: Google Blog / The Register / Engadget | **Category**: Models

Google DeepMind released Gemma 4 on April 2, a family of four open-weight models (E2B, E4B, 26B MoE, 31B Dense) built from the Gemini 3 architecture and licensed under Apache 2.0. The 31B Dense variant ranked third on Arena AI's text leaderboard, beating models 20× its size; the edge variants run fully offline on smartphones and single-board computers at up to 4× the speed and 60% lower battery draw than prior Gemma versions. With 256K context windows, native vision and audio, and 140+ language support, this is Google's most direct challenge yet to Meta's Llama family — and The Register frames it explicitly as a response to Chinese open-weight competition.

**Source**: TechCrunch / Microsoft Community Hub / GeekWire | **Category**: Models / Industry

### 3. OpenAI Closes $122 Billion Round at $852 Billion Valuation
**Source**: CNBC / Bloomberg / TechCrunch | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI closed the largest private fundraise in history on March 31, valuing the company at $852 billion. Amazon led with $50 billion (with $35B contingent on IPO or AGI milestone), followed by Nvidia and SoftBank at $30 billion each. Retail investors participated for the first time via bank channels, contributing $3 billion. OpenAI is generating $2 billion per month in revenue ($13.1B in 2025) but remains unprofitable. The round cements OpenAI as the highest-valued private startup on record and sets a precedent for retail-accessible pre-IPO AI investment.

### 5. GSA Draft AI Procurement Clause Draws Sharp Industry Pushback
**Source**: Nextgov/FCW / Gibson Dunn / Holland & Knight | **Category**: Policy

The GSA's draft clause GSAR 552.239-7001 — open for public comment through April 3 — would grant the federal government ownership of all input data and custom AI developments, plus rights to use contractor AI systems for "any lawful government purpose." Trade groups including TechNet warned the clause will deter commercial AI providers from federal contracting. Americans for Responsible Innovation flagged specific civil liberties risks: the provision is broad enough to cover psychological profiling of benefits applicants and employee loyalty screening. The comment period closed yesterday; the final rule's scope will determine whether the top commercial labs can realistically serve government customers.

### 6. Anthropic Restricts Claude Subscriptions to First-Party Use
**Source**: The Neuron | **Category**: Industry

Effective today (April 4, 12:00 PM PT), Anthropic's paid Claude subscriptions no longer cover usage on third-party integrations. The change is framed as capacity management, pushing third-party developers to direct API billing. It reflects the pressure Anthropic faces scaling compute to match its growth — the company's annualized revenue more than doubled in the first two months of 2026, driven primarily by Claude Code — and may accelerate consolidation among Claude-wrapper products that haven't yet moved to direct API arrangements.
