### 3. OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet for agent-led checkout
**Source**: TechCrunch, CNBC, The Paypers | **Category**: Industry

Launched Nov. 13, ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI's "dots" agents complete purchases on ordinary merchant sites without per-merchant integrations, using network tokenization so neither agent nor merchant sees the real card number. Rollout is limited to U.S. Pro subscribers (18+) with U.S.-issued Visa, Mastercard, or Amex, and OpenAI is taking no commission for now. The friction point is merchant resistance — Amazon blocks agent purchases and United Airlines said it will block agent bookings.

### 4. FAA nationwide ground stop puts new AI air traffic tool under scrutiny
**Source**: GeekWire, Washington Times, KXAN | **Category**: Policy

The FAA halted departures nationwide on Nov. 13, three days after expanding its AI-assisted SMART traffic-planning tool (built by Air Space Intelligence under an $875M, 12-year contract) to all 22 en-route centers. Officials blame aging telecom equipment, and Transportation Secretary Duffy says SMART played no role, though DHS Secretary Mullin declined to fully rule it out. More than 3,400 flights were cancelled by Saturday; the episode revives congressional warnings about deploying AI in safety-critical infrastructure.

### 5. Incoming Democratic Congress makes AI regulation a top priority
**Source**: NPR, Futurism | **Category**: Policy

After winning both chambers in the Nov. 3 midterms, congressional Democrats are preparing a wave of AI legislation aimed at frontier labs and the data centers that power them, setting up a clash with the administration's hands-off stance. Tighter AI chip export controls are also back on the agenda in the wake of the Nvidia spy scandal.

### 6. Dreamteam AI raises $53M to take on Salesforce and SAP

Dreamteam AI closed a $53M round led by Accel, with Premji Invest and Together Fund participating, to build an AI-native CRM platform. It is a further sign that investors see enterprise systems of record as open to displacement by agent-first entrants.
