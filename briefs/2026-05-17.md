## Top Stories

### 1. Trump Administration Reverses Course on AI Oversight
**Source**: Fortune / NPR / Washington Post | **Category**: Policy

After months of aggressively rolling back AI regulation, the Trump administration is now studying an executive order that would require advanced AI models to pass safety vetting before release — modeled loosely on FDA drug approval. The shift is driven by national security concerns around Anthropic's Mythos model and its demonstrated ability to identify and exploit cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Treasury Secretary Bessent framed the reversal plainly: "There is a very important calculus here between innovation and safety." An interagency battle is also underway over whether U.S. intelligence agencies should gain formal authority to evaluate AI models before public release.

### 3. OpenAI Consolidates Into a Super-App, Hits $25B ARR
**Source**: AI Tools Recap / OpenAI Newsroom | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI is merging ChatGPT, its Codex coding agent, and the developer API into a single unified product team with the explicit goal of building a "super app" — one that also integrates the Atlas browser. Separately, the company announced "Frontier," an enterprise platform for building, deploying, and managing AI agents with shared context and existing-system integrations. The consolidation comes as OpenAI reports $25 billion in annual recurring revenue, a figure that represents extraordinary growth but also enormous compute obligations: the Sora shutdown (see Story 5) illustrates how quickly those obligations can become untenable.

### 4. Cohere and Aleph Alpha Merge to Form Sovereign Enterprise AI Provider
**Source**: Crescendo AI / Blockchain Council | **Category**: Industry

Cohere (Canada) and Aleph Alpha (Germany) announced a merger, creating a transatlantic AI company explicitly positioned as a sovereign, enterprise-grade alternative to U.S. hyperscaler models. The combined entity targets regulated European and North American industries that require data residency, auditability, and independence from U.S. cloud infrastructure. The deal is the most structurally significant enterprise AI consolidation of the year.

### 5. OpenAI Shuts Down Sora Six Months After Launch
**Source**: MarketingProfs / imfounder | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI has discontinued Sora, its AI video-generation app, after a six-month run that saw initial enthusiasm collapse under brutal unit economics: the app burned an estimated $15 million per day in compute against lifetime revenue of just $2.1 million. Active users had fallen below 500,000. The shutdown also unwound a planned $1 billion Disney partnership. The episode is a pointed data point in a broader debate about whether consumer generative media products are a viable business at current infrastructure costs.

### 6. EU Agrees to Simplify AI Act Implementation
**Source**: European Commission / Transparency Coalition | **Category**: Policy

EU member states reached political agreement on May 7 on an "AI omnibus" package that streamlines AI Act compliance requirements, particularly for SMEs and general-purpose AI providers. The Commission simultaneously opened consultation on transparency obligation guidelines and agreed to ban "nudification" apps outright. The simplification is a partial concession to industry pressure but preserves the Act's risk-tier architecture and high-risk system prohibitions intact.

### 7. University of Pennsylvania Introduces "Mollifier Layers" for Scientific AI
**Source**: ScienceDaily / devFlokers | **Category**: Research

Penn Engineering researchers published "Mollifier Layers," a technique that embeds classical mathematical smoothing functions into neural networks to solve inverse partial differential equations with significantly greater stability on noisy real-world data — a longstanding failure mode in scientific AI. Applications span genomics, materials science, climate modeling, and chromatin biology. The paper is accepted to *Transactions on Machine Learning Research* and will be presented at NeurIPS 2026. It is one of several ICLR/NeurIPS 2026 submissions this week indicating a methodological maturation in physics-informed neural networks.
