## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic Ships Claude Sonnet 5 as New Default Model
**Source**: TechCrunch / 9to5Mac | **Category**: Models

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5 on June 30, making it the default for all Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise users immediately. The model delivers near-Opus 4.8 performance on knowledge work benchmarks at Sonnet pricing — introductory rates of $2/$10 per million input/output tokens through August 31, then moving to $3/$15 — and closes most of the agentic capability gap that previously required the heavier Opus tier. The launch coincides with Anthropic reporting approximately $30 billion in annualized revenue, edging past OpenAI's ~$25 billion and marking a striking benchmark shift for a company that only crossed $1B ARR in late 2024.

### 2. SpaceX Acquires Cursor's Parent Anysphere for $60 Billion
**Source**: TechCrunch / CNBC / Forbes | **Category**: Industry

SpaceX agreed to acquire Anysphere — parent company of the AI coding assistant Cursor — for $60 billion in stock on June 16, the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup ever and the deal closes the same week SpaceX's own IPO raised $86 billion on Nasdaq. Cursor had reached ~$4 billion in annualized revenue, ~$2.6 billion of it enterprise; the deal feeds Cursor's coding data into Grok's training pipeline and gives Cursor access to xAI's Colossus supercluster. The acquisition solidifies Musk's ambition for a vertically integrated AI development stack spanning models, compute, and developer tooling.

### 3. OpenAI Files Confidential S-1, Targeting IPO at $730B–$850B Valuation
**Source**: CNBC / Fortune / Inc. | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI filed a confidential S-1 with the SEC on June 8, targeting a potential public listing as early as Q4 2026 at a private-market valuation between $730 billion and $852 billion. The company is generating $2 billion in revenue per month — growing four times faster than Alphabet and Meta did at comparable stages — though internal projections show a $14 billion loss in 2026 and no profitability expected until 2029. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading underwriters. If completed, the OpenAI IPO would be among the largest in U.S. history; Anthropic is also reported to be targeting a public debut later this year.

### 4. Anthropic Introduces Identity Verification for Individual Claude Users
**Source**: Anthropic | **Category**: Policy

Starting July 8, Anthropic will begin rolling out government-issued ID upload and facial comparison for individual Claude users — a significant shift toward platform accountability ahead of both U.S. federal guidelines and the EU AI Act's August enforcement deadline. Business customers are exempt for now. The measure signals that frontier labs are hardening their identity posture in anticipation of tightening regulatory requirements, and raises immediate questions about anonymized research workflows and privacy implications in jurisdictions with strict biometric data laws.

### 5. Anthropic Secures Exclusive Compute at SpaceX's Colossus Facility
**Source**: AI Weekly | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic has locked in a deal to use the full compute capacity of Colossus 1, SpaceX/xAI's data center in Memphis — over 300 megawatts backed by more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs. The arrangement is notable because SpaceX simultaneously owns Cursor (a direct Anthropic competitor via the Anysphere acquisition), underscoring how infrastructure access now supersedes competitive dynamics in frontier AI. The deal makes Anthropic one of the largest external tenants of Colossus capacity.

### 6. OpenAI Retires GPT-4.5, Completes Transition to GPT-5 Family
**Source**: OpenAI / BleepingComputer | **Category**: Models

As of June 26, GPT-4.5 is no longer available in ChatGPT — completing OpenAI's transition away from the GPT-4 architecture in its consumer product. Existing conversations migrate automatically to GPT-5.5, which received a quality upgrade in May and is being tuned to produce more conversational, less bullet-heavy responses. The API is unaffected for now, but the retirement pattern signals where platform investment is concentrated ahead of the IPO.

### 7. Colorado AI Act Delayed and Substantially Weakened Before Taking Effect
**Source**: Norton Rose Fulbright / Troutman Privacy | **Category**: Policy

Colorado Governor Polis signed SB 189 on May 14, delaying the state's landmark AI Act from its June 30 effective date to January 1, 2027 and stripping it of its most ambitious provisions — including the duty of reasonable care to prevent algorithmic discrimination and mandatory risk management programs for high-risk AI deployers. What remains is a narrower disclosure-and-transparency framework. The reversal is a significant win for industry lobbying and tracks with the Trump administration's preference for federal preemption over state-level AI liability regimes.

### 8. EU AI Act Full Applicability in 32 Days
**Source**: European Commission | **Category**: Policy

The EU AI Act becomes fully applicable on August 2, 2026 — 32 days from today. The August 2 deadline activates obligations for high-risk AI systems: mandatory conformity assessments, detailed documentation requirements, and human oversight provisions for AI deployed in hiring, credit, healthcare, critical infrastructure, and law enforcement. Prohibited practices have been enforceable since February 2025; governance rules since August 2025. Companies that have not completed their EU-facing AI risk tier mapping are now in the red zone.
