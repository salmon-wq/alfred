## Top Stories

### 1. Nvidia halts AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries
**Source**: Financial Times (via Forbes, Nikkei Asia, TrendForce) | **Category**: Policy

Nvidia told manufacturing partners, including Dell and Supermicro, to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the FT reported on Nov 26. The halt follows a new Commerce Department rule on countries where Chinese firms are believed to rent data-centre compute, and comes after Bloomberg traced a restricted GB300 NVL72 Blackwell rack to a Shanghai data centre. It is unclear whether the halt is temporary while partners work through compliance.

### 2. OpenAI fixes GPT-6.2 billing error, and ChatGPT limits now run out sooner
**Source**: The Next Web, InfoWorld, gHacks | **Category**: Models

On Nov 26, OpenAI said a billing mistake had under-counted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage since their Nov 17 launch. After the fix, subscribers hit their limits about 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol. OpenAI will not back-charge past usage and is giving Go, Plus, Pro and Business subscribers one banked limit reset. Effectively a price increase, it shows how much model "value" depends on opaque metering.

### 3. Microsoft and FBI warn that state hackers are using the ARTEX agentic attack framework
**Source**: Microsoft Security Blog, The Register, Help Net Security, BleepingComputer | **Category**: Policy

Microsoft's Nov 25 report names six actors, including Salt Typhoon, Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet, using ARTEX, an open-source framework where AI agents plan and run reconnaissance, credential attacks and web exploitation. Microsoft says it adds no new techniques but speeds up attacks: it can go from scanning to privileged access in minutes. The FBI separately warned that ARTEX lets low-skill operators run several targets at once. It remains available through forks and a commercial "ARTEX PRO" edition.

### 4. FCC conditionally approves SpaceX's one-million-satellite "Starmind" orbital compute constellation
**Source**: CNBC, TechCrunch, CNN | **Category**: Industry

The FCC gave SpaceX conditional approval on Nov 25 to deploy up to 1 million satellites for orbital AI data centres. SpaceX has 7 years to put its first operational satellite in orbit and 14 years to finish the constellation, and is targeting first launches in Q3 2027. The FCC has not yet approved debris plans for satellites above 600 km, and Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society opposed the application.

### 5. Canada expands its AI Compute Access Fund to C$1 billion
**Source**: The Next Web, Digital Watch | **Category**: Policy

Minister Evan Solomon announced a C$700M top-up on Nov 26, bringing the fund to C$1B. It covers up to 80% of eligible costs on Canadian-owned "sovereign" clouds, versus 50% on other in-country providers, for firms with fewer than 500 staff. The first call targets health and life sciences. It is an explicit industrial-policy push to keep startup compute spending in Canada.

### 6. Huawei begins shipping Ascend 950 chips and Atlas 950 systems to South Korea
**Source**: DigiTimes (via Huawei Central) | **Category**: Industry

Huawei has reportedly started shipping Ascend 950PR (inference) and 950DT (training) chips, plus the Atlas 950 SuperPoD, to South Korea, with SK Shieldus expected to handle distribution. It is Huawei's first overseas push for Ascend into a market dominated by Nvidia. Its performance-per-price claims against Nvidia's H20 come from Huawei and have not been independently verified.

### 7. Anthropic expands Claude Duet agent beyond the US
**Source**: tbreak, BigGo Finance | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic's proactive agent app Claude Duet, a research preview that triages email, calendar and tools, opened to invited Pro, Max and Ultra users in India, Brazil, Indonesia and Japan on Nov 25–26. It launched in the US on Oct 21. There is still no date for Europe or the UK, and Enterprise plans are not yet included.

- **Compute geopolitics are getting stricter**: US export controls now reach third-country data centres, and Huawei is moving into markets where Nvidia is constrained.
