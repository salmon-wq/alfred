### 1. Apple Sues OpenAI for Trade Secret Theft
**Source**: CNBC, Bloomberg, TechCrunch | **Category**: Industry

Apple filed suit in federal court in the Northern District of California on July 10, alleging that OpenAI systematically stole its AI hardware design trade secrets — specifically naming Tang Tan, a former Apple VP now serving as OpenAI's Chief Hardware Officer. The complaint alleges Tan directed Apple employees interviewing at OpenAI to bring proprietary hardware to "show and tell" sessions as a condition of the process. The lawsuit is a remarkable reversal of a high-profile 2024 partnership that embedded ChatGPT into iOS; OpenAI responded: "We have no interest in other companies' trade secrets."

### 2. OpenAI GPT-5.6 (Sol, Terra, Luna) Reaches General Availability
**Source**: OpenAI, Simon Willison | **Category**: Models

OpenAI released its GPT-5.6 family to general availability on July 9 across ChatGPT, Codex, and the API. Sol Pro is reserved for the most demanding Pro and Enterprise workloads. GPT-5.5 Instant remains the everyday default for speed-sensitive tasks.

### 3. FTC Targets State AI Accuracy Laws; EU AI Act Transparency Rules Incoming
**Source**: FTC, Federal Register, TechPolicy.Press | **Category**: Policy

The FTC published a policy statement (Federal Register docket 2026-13628, July 7) targeting state laws that would compel AI companies to alter truthful model outputs, framing such mandates as accuracy suppression. Public comment is open until July 31. Separately, the EU AI Act's transparency provisions are scheduled to activate in August 2026. On the state level, US jurisdictions have now enacted 109 AI laws and 28 data center laws, though broad algorithmic discrimination protections in Colorado and California are being significantly scaled back.
