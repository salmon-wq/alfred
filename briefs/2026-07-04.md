### 2. OpenAI and Broadcom Unveil Jalapeño — OpenAI's First Custom Inference Chip
**Source**: OpenAI / CNBC / VentureBeat | **Category**: Models / Industry

OpenAI and Broadcom jointly unveiled Jalapeño on June 24, a reticle-sized ASIC optimized for LLM inference, designed from clean-sheet to tape-out in nine months — potentially the fastest ASIC development cycle ever recorded in high-performance silicon. Engineering samples are running ML workloads at production frequency. The chip is targeted for initial deployment by end of 2026, with Microsoft and other cloud partners lined up. The strategic subtext: reducing dependence on Nvidia at scale and creating a vertically integrated inference stack.

### 3. GPT-5.6 Held at the Gate by U.S. Government — a Historic First
**Source**: TechTimes / AlphaMatch | **Category**: Models / Policy

OpenAI previewed GPT-5.6 (a three-tier family: Sol, Terra, Luna) on June 26, then immediately delayed its full public rollout at the formal request of the Office of the National Cyber Director and the Office of Science and Technology Policy. This is the first instance of the U.S. government formally staging the release of a frontier AI model on national security grounds — a precedent with significant implications for how powerful future models will be introduced. Daily technical discussions between OpenAI and federal officials are ongoing.

### 4. Qualcomm Acquires Modular for ~$4B to Challenge Nvidia's CUDA Grip
**Source**: CNBC / Bloomberg | **Category**: Industry

Qualcomm announced on June 24 the acquisition of AI software startup Modular in an all-stock deal valued at approximately $3.92 billion. Modular built a hardware-neutral AI execution layer that runs models across Nvidia, AMD, and other chips without per-processor rewrites — a direct attack on the developer lock-in that has made CUDA central to Nvidia's $5T dominance. The deal is expected to close in the second half of 2026 and signals Qualcomm's intent to compete in the data center AI software stack, not just at the edge.

### 7. U.S. State AI Law Surge: 19 New Laws in 11 States in Two Weeks

### 8. EU AI Act Full Implementation Deadline: August 2, 2026
**Source**: European Commission / Holland & Knight | **Category**: Policy

The EU AI Act reaches full applicability on August 2, 2026, imposing hard compliance requirements for high-risk AI systems across the European market. Transparency mandates for general-purpose AI models have been in force since August 2025; the August 2 deadline adds conformity assessments, human oversight requirements, and incident reporting obligations for high-risk applications. Companies without completed impact assessments face enforcement exposure in less than 30 days.
