## Top Stories

### 1. Illinois Signs the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act
**Source**: Capitol News Illinois / NPR Illinois | **Category**: Policy

Governor JB Pritzker signed the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act on July 6, the strictest state-level AI law in the US to date. It targets "large frontier developers" generating at least $500M in annual revenue, requiring annual independent third-party audits — a first-in-the-nation mandate — 72-hour incident reporting (24 hours if an imminent threat of death or injury), and published safety frameworks defining "catastrophic risk" (events causing death or serious injury to more than 50 people, or property damage exceeding $1M). Both OpenAI and Anthropic supported the bill; it passed unanimously in the Illinois House. The law takes effect January 1, 2028.

### 2. UN Convenes First Intergovernmental AI Governance Dialogue in Geneva
**Source**: UNESCO / UN News | **Category**: Policy

The United Nations opened its inaugural Global Dialogue on AI Governance in Geneva on July 6–7, with all 193 member states in attendance — the first intergovernmental AI summit where developing nations hold full standing rather than observer status. Secretary-General António Guterres issued an urgent call warning of "catastrophic harm" from uncontrolled AI, and from autonomous weapons specifically. Expected outputs include agreed governance principles, a multilateral dialogue mechanism, and potentially an information-sharing framework for AI safety incidents. Final communiqué documents had not been published as of this morning.

### 3. OpenAI Previews GPT-5.6 Sol on Cerebras Hardware at 750 Tokens/Second
**Source**: OpenAI / VentureBeat | **Category**: Models

OpenAI previewed the GPT-5.6 family — three tiers named Sol, Terra, and Luna — limited to approximately 20 partner organizations under US government review. In July, OpenAI is expanding Sol onto Cerebras wafer-scale hardware targeting 750 tokens/second, roughly 15× current GPU inference throughput. Pricing tiers: Sol at $5/$30 per 1M tokens (input/output), Terra at $2.50/$15, Luna at $1/$6. Broad general availability is targeted for "mid-to-late July" but no specific GA date has been confirmed.

### 4. Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 Returns After 19-Day Export Control Suspension
**Source**: CNBC / Anthropic | **Category**: Models

Anthropic redeployed Claude Fable 5 globally on July 1 after the Trump administration lifted an export control order imposed June 12, following Amazon researchers publishing a jailbreak method that enabled the model to identify software vulnerabilities and generate exploit code. The redeployment includes a new cybersecurity safety classifier with a reported >99% block rate against the specific technique. As of July 7, Fable 5 shifts from included usage to usage-credit billing for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscribers.

### 5. Gemini 3.5 Pro Still in Preview, GA Now Targeting July 17
**Source**: BigGo Finance / Bind AI Blog | **Category**: Models

Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro entered the second week of July still in enterprise preview, having slipped from its June GA target after testers flagged weaknesses in coding, token efficiency, and long-horizon multi-step reasoning. Google opted for a full architectural rebuild rather than patching the existing 2.5 Pro base — adding a 2M token context window, a "Deep Think Reasoning Layer," and improved SVG and image generation. The revised launch target is July 17. Notably, four senior Google DeepMind researchers departed for Anthropic during the delay period.

### 6. Together AI Raises $800M Series C at $8.3B Valuation
**Source**: TechCrunch / BusinessWire | **Category**: Industry

Open-source inference neocloud Together AI closed an $800M Series C on July 1 at an $8.3B valuation — more than double its $3.3B Series B valuation from 16 months prior. The round was led by Aramco Ventures with participation from Vista Equity Partners, General Catalyst, Emergence Capital, Nvidia, March Capital, Pegatron, and SentinelOne's S Ventures. Annual bookings surpassed $1.15B last quarter, driven by surging enterprise demand for open-source inference as an alternative to closed-model API lock-in. Proceeds will fund a targeted ~50× infrastructure expansion over five years.
