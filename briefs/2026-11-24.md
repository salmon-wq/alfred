### 1. Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, Stock Jumps 31%

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on November 23 to bring Huang back as president, CEO and director, eleven days after he resigned on November 12. ### 2. Anthropic Launches Claude Ship for AI-Driven Code Review and Approvals
**Source**: TechCrunch | **Category**: Models / Product

Claude Ship bundles three GitHub agents: Code Review (generally available), plus PR Steward and Claude Approvals (both beta). Approvals can sign off on routine pull requests only under a policy file each team writes, and Anthropic recommends starting in observe-only mode; existing branch protections still decide what merges. It is rolling out to Team and Enterprise plans and is not available to Zero Data Retention, HIPAA or customer-managed-key orgs. This is the furthest an AI lab has gone toward automating code approval itself.

### 3. Amazon Pays $7.5B for Decart's Technology and Team
**Source**: Haaretz / Calcalist | **Category**: Industry

Amazon agreed to acquire the technology behind Decart's optimization stack and hire its founders and engineers in a deal reported at $7.5 billion, the largest ever for an Israeli AI company. It is structured as a tech-and-talent deal, not a purchase of Decart itself, and comes at nearly twice the roughly $4 billion valuation from Decart's $300M round in May. The "acqui-license" structure keeps spreading as big tech tries to avoid full merger review.

### 4. Oregon and Virginia Sue SpaceX Over Grok Sexual Deepfakes
**Source**: CNBC / Engadget | **Category**: Policy

The first state lawsuits over Grok's image tools were filed November 23 under consumer protection laws. They allege that Grok's "spicy mode" let users turn real photos into nude or sexual images, including of minors, and that the company misrepresented Grok and X as safe. Defendants include SpaceX Super Intelligence, the former xAI, and X Corp. Virginia cites estimates of more than 20,000 images involving minors. Baltimore, the California AG and the UK's media regulator are pursuing separate actions.

### 5. U.S. Data-Center Export Rule Takes Effect
**Source**: The Collectives / OriginBrief policy roundups | **Category**: Policy

A Commerce Department rule now requires a U.S. license for data centers anywhere in the world that supply large amounts of advanced compute to companies headquartered in China and other restricted countries. This moves export controls from shipping chips to selling compute as a service, closing the cloud-rental loophole. *Note: reported by policy aggregators; primary Federal Register text not verified for this brief.*

## Key Themes
- **Government influence on AI companies is now direct**: The White House shaped Nvidia's leadership decision, Commerce is regulating compute rentals, and state AGs are suing labs over product safety.
- **Agents are moving from writing code to approving it**: Claude Ship gives an AI the approval step, but only behind team policy files and human-set branch rules.
- **Talent deals keep getting bigger**: Amazon's $7.5B for Decart shows non-acquisition tech-and-talent deals remain the preferred M&A route.
- **State enforcement is outpacing federal law**: Oregon, Virginia, New York (whose RAISE Act enforcement unit starts Jan 1) and California are acting while federal frameworks such as the SAFE AI Act are still in outline.
