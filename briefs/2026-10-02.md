# AI News Brief — 2026-10-02

## Overview
Frontier labs are colliding with oversight from every direction at once: OpenAI fires three safety researchers, Washington inserts itself ahead of U.K. model testers, and Anthropic readies a trillion-dollar-scale IPO.

## Top Stories

### 1. OpenAI Fires Three Safety Researchers Over Alleged Information Sharing
**Source**: The Wall Street Journal (via Fox Business, TechTimes, Technology.org) | **Category**: Policy

OpenAI dismissed three members of its safety division for "mishandling sensitive information outside established company procedures," reportedly by sharing it with an outside AI-safety organization; OpenAI has not named the people, the group, or the data. One of the three says they were OpenAI's contact for the METR investigation into its Hugging Face hack. The firings sharpen an existing question: whether safety staff who escalate concerns have any sanctioned channel to do so.

### 2. Anthropic Targets Mid-November IPO at Up to $2 Trillion
**Source**: PYMNTS, Investing.com, Yahoo Finance | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic reportedly plans to begin IPO marketing as early as the week of Nov. 9 and start trading before Thanksgiving (Nov. 26), with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citigroup as lead underwriters. Prospective investors put the valuation at $1.8–2 trillion; reported revenue run rate climbed from $9B at end-2025 to $65B by end of July, against an operating loss of more than $8B. The timing remains subject to change.

### 3. White House Asks OpenAI and Anthropic to Hold Models From U.K. Testers Pending U.S. Review
**Source**: The Next Web, The Decoder, Quartz | **Category**: Policy

The Office of the National Cyber Director has asked both labs not to share new frontier models with the U.K. AI Security Institute until U.S. government testing is complete; Anthropic has already withheld Mythos 5.1, limiting it to "a set of U.S. organizations." AISI director Henry de Zoete told Parliament the institute still has pre-release access to some frontier models, including OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra. The move ends the direct lab-to-ally evaluation pipeline and gives Washington first look.

### 4. OpenAI DevDay 2026: Dots Agents and GPT-6.1 Sol
**Source**: Business Standard, Latent Space, AlternativeTo | **Category**: Models

At DevDay (Sept. 29) OpenAI launched Dots, always-on agents powered by GPT-6 Astra, each with its own cloud computer and browser, access to 4,000+ apps, and presence across ChatGPT, Slack, Teams, email and text. GPT-6.1 Sol targets agentic coding and computer use at near-Astra performance for roughly one-fifth of Astra's token price, alongside a new Decisions API for bounded approve/reject/escalate choices. The pitch is plain: OpenAI wants to own the workflow, not just rent the model.

### 5. AMD to Acquire Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2 Billion
**Source**: CNBC, Bloomberg, AMD Newsroom | **Category**: Industry

AMD agreed to buy world-model developer World Labs in an all-stock deal valued at about $8.2B, expected to close by end of 2026 pending regulatory approval; Fei-Fei Li becomes EVP and chief scientist reporting to Lisa Su. It is AMD's second-largest acquisition after Xilinx (~$50B). The bet is that 3D world simulation, not just text LLMs, will shape the next generation of AI silicon.

### 6. Newsom Signs 13 AI Bills, Including a Ban on "Robo Bosses"
**Source**: CNBC, CalMatters, Healthcare IT News | **Category**: Policy

California now requires human review of AI-driven firing or discipline decisions, notice when AI causes mass layoffs, limits on workplace surveillance, and new deepfake protections; AB 1979 restricts healthcare AI to an advisory role. Newsom vetoed two labor-backed bills, one protecting healthcare workers who override or rely on AI output and one limiting AI in mental healthcare. Separately, a Connecticut AI and chatbot law took effect Oct. 1.

### 7. DeepMind Signals Software-First Robotics Strategy; Gemini 4 Coming in Stages
**Source**: GuruFocus, Dataconomy | **Category**: Research

Google DeepMind CEO Koray Kavukcuoglu said the company will prioritize robotics intelligence and software over building its own humanoid hardware. That follows DeepMind's statement that Gemini 4 will arrive earlier than expected, shipped as an early version and then iterated in public instead of held back for a polished launch.
