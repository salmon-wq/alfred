### 1. Moonshot AI Releases Kimi K3 — World's Largest Open-Source Model
**Source**: VentureBeat, MarkTechPost, Fortune | **Category**: Models / Research

Chinese AI lab Moonshot AI released Kimi K3 on July 16: a 2.8-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with a novel "Kimi Delta Attention" hybrid linear attention mechanism and a native 1-million-token context window — the largest open-source model ever shipped. On GDPval-AA v2 (a real-world task benchmark across 44 occupations), Kimi K3 scored 1,687, placing third globally behind Claude Fable 5 Max (1,815) and GPT-5.6 Sol Max (1,747.8). Full weights drop July 27, which will allow independent evaluation and fine-tuning at a scale not previously possible in the open-source community.

### 2. 29 Nations Found WAICO — China-Led Global AI Governance Body
**Source**: PYMNTS, The Next Web, People's Daily | **Category**: Policy

Twenty-nine nations — including Russia, Brazil, Pakistan, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, and Laos — signed the founding agreement for the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO) in Shanghai on July 16, structured as an independent international body under UN Charter principles. Chinese President Xi Jinping attended the 2026 World AI Conference (WAIC, July 17–20) opening in person for the first time in the event's history. WAICO's stated mission is to promote "beneficial, safe, and fair" AI and to build AI capacity in Global South countries — a direct counter-architecture to the US-led AI Safety Institute network and the EU AI Act's extraterritorial reach.

### 4. FTC: Secretly Steering AI Outputs May Violate Federal Consumer Protection Law
**Source**: FTC.gov, AI Weekly, Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor | **Category**: Policy

The FTC issued a proposed policy statement on July 1 taking the position that AI companies secretly steering model outputs toward undisclosed ideological or commercial objectives — rather than toward the most accurate response — may constitute consumer deception under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The agency cites its own data that consumers accept AI outputs without fact-checking more than 90% of the time, making accuracy expectations a material reliance claim. The statement is partly a response to Colorado's AI Act. Public comment period closes July 31 — companies shipping production AI systems should engage.
