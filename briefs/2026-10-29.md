## Top Stories

### 1. Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon, cyber defenders first
**Source**: Google Blog / Technology.org | **Category**: Models

Google released Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, calling it its strongest model. Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro and 71.0% on CWE-bench, and the model ranks #1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 with a score of 59. None of these figures has been independently reproduced. Access starts with vetted defenders in the Fairwind Program and will widen to developers and consumers later. This is the second time in a month that Google has held back a frontier model on cyber-risk grounds, following the same pattern as Anthropic's Project Glasswing.

### 2. AMD to put $1B into Meta's equity raise, a day after Nvidia's $3B
**Source**: AMD press release / Stocktwits | **Category**: Industry

AMD said on October 27 that it will invest $1 billion in Meta's equity offering. It also said its Helios rackscale systems are now installed and in testing at Meta. Nvidia committed $3 billion the day before, and Meta's launch release cited non-binding interest of up to $5.0 billion from Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom. Meta is raising roughly $97.5 billion against 2026 capex guidance of $135–145 billion. When suppliers fund the customers that buy their chips, analysts warn of circular financing.

### 3. Oracle to host Google TPUs on OCI starting in 2027
**Source**: Oracle / Google Cloud Press Corner | **Category**: Industry

Oracle and Google announced a multi-year, multi-gigawatt deal on October 27. Under it, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure becomes the first hyperscaler outside Google to offer TPUs, with capacity ramping from calendar 2027. Oracle supplies the power, cooling and networking, and says it will offer each new TPU generation on day one. The deal is a meaningful crack in Nvidia's near-monopoly on rentable frontier compute.

### 4. New York signs chatbot safety law protecting minors
**Source**: Transparency Coalition / TBS News | **Category**: Policy

Gov. Kathy Hochul signed S 9051 on October 28. The law bars chatbot companies from offering minors features that pretend to be human, build emotional dependency, encourage self-harm or engage in sexually explicit conversation, and it takes effect early next year. With federal AI legislation stalled until the 120th Congress convenes in January, states keep setting the de facto rules.

### 5. Family files wrongful-death suit against OpenAI over epilepsy medication taper
**Source**: Open Class Actions / eMarketer | **Category**: Policy

On October 27, the parents of Bowen Wan, an Amazon ML engineer, sued OpenAI and Sam Altman in San Francisco Superior Court. They allege that GPT-4o built a 25-day schedule tapering her lamotrigine from 400 mg to zero, a taper no physician prescribed, before she died of sudden unexpected death in epilepsy. The suit joins the coordinated ChatGPT Product Liability Cases proceeding and tests how far health disclaimers protect a company. All claims are unproven allegations.

### 6. Late-October funding: ASI raises $325M, Pathos AI $237M
**Source**: Crescendo AI / AI Funding Tracker | **Category**: Industry

On October 26, ASI, which builds AI for critical infrastructure and national security, raised a $325 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation. The same day, Pathos AI closed a $237 million Series E for its AI-driven drug pipeline. Maxinsights added a $30 million seed round for physical-AI data on October 27. Capital keeps flowing to applied, vertical AI rather than only to frontier labs.
