## Top Stories

### 1. OpenAI Trims Funding-Round Valuation Target to $1.25 Trillion
**Source**: Financial Times / CNBC (via Seeking Alpha, Tech Funding News) | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI has reportedly cut the pre-money target for its new round of up to $30 billion from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion after investor demand was weaker than in previous rounds; Coatue and UAE-based funds are discussing leading it, with commitments due October 30. The figure is still roughly 50% above March's $852 billion valuation, but it is the first public sign of price resistance at the frontier, and it lands in a week when Nasdaq is under pressure from fears about AI capital spending. The details come from anonymous sources, not company statements.

### 2. Microsoft: China-Aligned Storm-0940 Used Agentic AI to Run the ConnectWise Supply-Chain Attack
**Source**: Microsoft (via Reuters, BleepingComputer) | **Category**: Policy

Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise to Storm-0940. The group abused a trusted OAuth app credential to bypass MFA and used the ARTEX agentic penetration-testing framework to automate reconnaissance and exfiltration across hundreds of organisations. One write-up of Microsoft's report puts the reach at more than 2,250 tenants, with targeting-to-privileged-access in under 3 hours in some cases. Huntress separately confirmed more than 300 successful compromises. This is one of the clearest documented cases of a state actor running an intrusion campaign at scale with agentic AI.

### 3. DeepSeek Reportedly Closes a ~100 Billion Yuan (~$15B) Round
**Source**: Reuters (via Express Tribune, Tech Startups) | **Category**: Industry

DeepSeek is reportedly wrapping up a raise of about 100 billion yuan, roughly $15 billion. Tencent and CATL are among the largest investors, and GigaDevice, Meituan, and Geely are joining. Earlier October reporting had put the round at "at least" 80 billion yuan, and the reported valuation varies by source, from around $52 billion implied by filings to $74 billion. It would be one of the largest private raises by a Chinese AI lab, and some reports tie it to a possible IPO.

### 4. Anthropic Launches Claude Duet, a Proactive Background Agent
**Source**: Anthropic (via iPhone in Canada, Trending Topics) | **Category**: Models

Announced October 21 as a waitlisted research preview for US Pro and Max subscribers, Duet runs on Claude Opus 5.5. It monitors connected apps, sends a morning brief, checks in every few hours, and asks for approval before consequential actions such as sending messages, making payments, or deleting things. It puts Anthropic directly against Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots in the always-on assistant market. Anthropic warns that background work counts against existing usage limits and that Duet can still misjudge what is urgent.

### 5. Big Banks Wall Off Third-Party AI Agents From Customer Accounts
**Source**: Financial Times (via Retail Banker International, Digital Today) | **Category**: Industry

Citi, Bank of America, JPMorgan, and Charles Schwab are restricting or blocking outside AI agents from logging into customer accounts or moving money. Bank of America's Brian Moynihan said access to deposits will stay limited until someone can determine who bears responsibility for payments an agent approves. The stated worries are liability and "deposit flight", meaning assistants that automatically move household cash into higher-yielding accounts. The consumer-agent products launched this month are already running into the institutions they need to reach.

### 6. Italy's EU AI Act Implementing Decree Takes Effect Today; China Proposes AI Export Controls
**Source**: TLT / Lexology / AI Governance Weekly | **Category**: Policy

Italy's decree implementing the EU AI Act and national Law 132/2025 takes effect October 23. Fines for prohibited practices run up to €35 million or 7% of turnover, which makes Italy one of the first member states with a fully specified enforcement regime. Separately, Beijing has proposed export controls covering 23 AI technologies, including large models and agents, and has exempted anything already released as open source or published academically. Comments close in mid-November.

### 7. Research: Where Reasoning Habits Come From, and Neurons That Do Make Sense
**Source**: The Neuron (HKUST-led paper; Goodfire; Microsoft) | **Category**: Research

A HKUST-led study found that 17 of 18 "zero-RL" training runs stayed below 0.05 occurrences of "wait" per 1,000 tokens. The finding suggests that reasoning models' self-correction markers come mostly from pretraining data rather than from reinforcement learning. Goodfire reported evidence that individual neurons encode more coherent functions than interpretability researchers had assumed. Microsoft also described a diagnostic system that outperformed general physicians on difficult medical cases. All three come from a daily digest and have not yet been checked against the primary papers.
