## Top Stories

### 1. Marvell to acquire Lumentum in $115B all-stock deal
**Source**: CBS News / The Information / Quartz (Reuters) | **Category**: Industry

Marvell agreed today to buy the laser and optical-components maker Lumentum at 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share. That is a 23.9% premium to the October 28 close and about $115B in enterprise value ($102.5B in equity, per Reuters). Closing is expected in the first half of 2028, pending shareholder and regulatory approval. This is one of the largest chip mergers on record. It signals that the bottleneck in AI clusters is moving from compute to the optical interconnect between chips, and that suppliers are consolidating around it.

### 2. OpenAI plans to lobby Congress for mandatory "speed limits" on frontier AI
**Source**: Axios (via Implicator, AI Weekly) | **Category**: Policy

OpenAI's chief global affairs officer, Chris Lehane, is preparing a Washington policy and PR campaign timed to the lame-duck session that opens November 9. It would push for mandatory safeguards on the most powerful AI systems, which the company calls "speed limits." The same package would rebrand data centers as "AI compute campuses" and back state laws giving communities a say in new buildouts. No legislative text has been published yet. A frontier lab asking for binding rules is notable at a time when federal policy has leaned toward deregulation and pre-empting state laws.

### 3. Data-center moratorium backers could win up to 15 governor races on Tuesday
**Source**: The Information (via Seoul Economic Daily) / Time | **Category**: Policy

The Information found that in 21 of the 36 governor races, at least one candidate supports pausing data-center construction. In five states (Michigan, New Hampshire, Ohio, Oregon and Texas), both major-party candidates do. Ohio's Vivek Ramaswamy and Amy Acton have each proposed some form of moratorium, and Texas already has a temporary pause in place pending an audit. Local resistance over power, water and cost has become one of the most concrete political constraints on AI infrastructure, and it is now on the ballot.

### 4. Anthropic extends its Claude text watermark to four older models tomorrow
**Source**: FourWeekMBA; background from Anthropic and Search Engine Journal | **Category**: Policy

FourWeekMBA reports that from November 3, Anthropic will apply its statistical text watermark to Claude Mythos 5, Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Opus 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 4.6. Those models were released before the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules took effect on August 2. Anthropic said in August that it would apply the watermark worldwide because it cannot yet limit it by region, so this change reaches users outside the EU too. The specific date and model list come from a single secondary source and are unconfirmed. The change comes as Anthropic's IPO is pending: its public S-1, filed October 26 for a Nasdaq listing as "ANT", still leaves the price range blank.

### 5. Gemini 4 Carbon reaches cyber defenders this week
**Source**: Bernama / BetaNews / Constellation Research | **Category**: Models

Google's Gemini 4 Carbon, announced October 28, is a frontier model built for finding vulnerabilities, testing whether they can be exploited and writing patches. Google reports about twice the secure-code success rate of its predecessor, Gemini 4 Argon. Access goes first to vetted defenders through Google's Fairwind programme, at the same introductory price as Argon ($2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens), and no general-availability date has been given. It is another frontier lab using staged release for capabilities that help attackers as much as defenders.

### 6. Apple Intelligence on Alibaba's Qwen due in China developer beta on November 4
**Source**: Phemex; background from EqualOcean and Reuters | **Category**: Industry

Apple will reportedly open its first Apple Intelligence features in mainland China for testing on November 4 through the latest iOS developer beta, built on Alibaba's Qwen models. A full rollout would follow with iOS 27.3 in December. Chinese regulators approved the service in July, but earlier reports pointed to an October beta, so the timing has already slipped once. This would be Apple's first large-scale shipment of a non-Apple foundation model as the core of Apple Intelligence in a major market.
