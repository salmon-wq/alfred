# AI News Brief — 2026-10-13

## Overview
Meta has its first frontier model priced above GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5, and an early chatbot unicorn has gone bankrupt. Broadcom's CEO said AI investing looks "almost like a casino," and Asian chip stocks fell sharply.

## Top Stories

### 1. Meta releases Muse Wonder, its first frontier model, at a premium price
**Source**: Meta Newsroom / TechCrunch / CNBC / OpenRouter | **Category**: Models

On October 12, Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which are built for agentic work: planning, delegating to sub-agents, calling tools in parallel and recovering from errors. Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens on the Meta Model API, double GPT-6.1 Sol. Wonder Full is available only to Muse Code subscribers. OpenRouter lists a 1M-token context window. Meta claims state-of-the-art agentic results and leads on its own MemoryBench, but it published no numeric benchmark scores, no parameter count and no open weights. Until independent evaluations exist, "world's best" is only Meta's claim.

### 2. Character.AI files for Chapter 11; Disney bids $20M for IP rights
**Source**: Reuters (via WTVB, Benzinga, Technology.org) / Character.AI blog | **Category**: Industry

Character.AI filed for voluntary Chapter 11 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on October 12, listing assets and liabilities of $50M–$100M. Disney is the stalking-horse bidder (its offer sets the floor for competing bids) at $20M. The bid covers a non-exclusive, perpetual license to certain IP and claims, not the chatbot platform. The app keeps running during the sale and will be wound down if no buyer takes it on. The sale is expected to close in late November or early December. Google paid $2.7B in its 2024 deal for Character.AI's founders and staff.

### 3. Hock Tan's "casino" remark triggers an Asian chip-stock sell-off
**Source**: CNBC TV18 / Startup Fortune / KuCoin News | **Category**: Markets

At a Singapore event on October 12, Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said many AI investors set extreme expectations and end up "almost like they are in a casino." He added that financing may now constrain the AI buildout more than chip supply does. On October 13, Korea's Kospi fell about 6%. Reports disagree on Samsung's drop: one says 7.4%, another says 13.39%, its worst day since 2008. SK Hynix fell about 10%. Unverified claims of a memory breakthrough at China's CXMT added to the selling. Check market data before relying on any of these percentages.

### 4. Anthropic says new defenses cut distillation harvesting by 90–99%
**Source**: Anthropic Research / Yahoo Finance | **Category**: Safety

Distillation attacks are covert, large-scale efforts to copy a model's capabilities by harvesting its outputs. Anthropic says its new two-stage classifier has a false-positive rate below 0.1% and that it has banned tens of thousands of accounts tied to these campaigns. It named Z.ai (Zhipu) as one attempted distiller. The 90–99% reduction is Anthropic's own early estimate. Separately, Nikkei reports that NTT Data is in final talks to build Anthropic's first Japanese data center in Osaka: up to 500 MW and up to ¥1T (~$6.3B), with partial operations as early as 2028. NTT declined to comment.

### 5. FT: Chinese firms bought 1M+ Nvidia gaming and workstation GPUs this year
**Source**: Financial Times (via Techmeme, Yahoo Finance) | **Category**: Industry

The FT reports that Chinese tech companies bought more than 1 million Nvidia RTX 5090 and RTX Pro cards to run basic AI workloads. US export rules do not block these products. Industry estimates put China's domestic AI-chip supply at about 3.5M units this year against demand above 5M, so even with these purchases the market remains short. This fits with Beijing reportedly weighing approval for ByteDance and Alibaba to buy RTX Pro 5500s (The Information, September).

Four days after the Super Intelligence Force's October 9 statement, there is still no published legal authority, definition of a reportable incident, deadline or penalty. ## Key Themes
- **Pricing as positioning**: Meta pricing Muse Wonder at twice GPT-6.1 Sol, with a $20/$100 consumer tier, is a bet that agentic capability will command a premium. Without published benchmarks, buyers have to take that on trust.
- **Capital is the constraint**: Tan's warning about financing and the Character.AI bankruptcy both suggest that money, not chips, is now what limits AI companies. Meanwhile China's shortage shows demand still outstrips supply.
- **Labs as self-regulators**: Anthropic's distillation defenses and its earlier incident disclosures come while the federal mandate has no enforcement terms. The labs are effectively writing the operating rules themselves.
