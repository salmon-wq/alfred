## Top Stories

### 1. Meta Superintelligence Labs ships Muse Wonder
**Source**: AI Tools Recap / AI Weekly | **Category**: Models

On October 12, Meta released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, priced at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. Meta disclosed no parameter count, context window or numeric benchmarks, so the price is the only hard number. Meta is moving away from open weights toward a paid frontier API.

### 2. Amazon in advanced talks to buy Decart for ~$7B
**Source**: Wall Street Journal (via Tech Startups, Seeking Alpha) | **Category**: Industry

Amazon is reportedly close to acquiring Decart, a San Francisco AI startup backed by Nvidia, for about $7 billion. That is nearly double the ~$4B valuation from Decart's May round. The deal is not final and another bidder could still emerge. If it closes, it adds to a string of large AI acquisitions after SpaceX–Cursor and Nvidia–Hugging Face.

### 3. Global chip sell-off after Broadcom CEO's "casino" remark
**Source**: Financial Times (via AI Weekly) | **Category**: Industry

On October 12, Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said many AI investors "have no clue" what they are buying. A global chip sell-off followed, with SK Hynix down as much as 9.2%, Samsung 8.8% and Micron 4%. Coming from an AI hardware supplier's own chief executive, the remark is a notable sign of worry about an AI bubble.

### 4. Microsoft unveils Maia 300 accelerator
**Source**: AI Weekly | **Category**: Industry

Microsoft announced Maia 300, its in-house AI chip, with 288 GB of HBM4 memory and 18 TB/s of bandwidth. Microsoft claims it delivers 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems. In-house chips at the large cloud providers keep growing as a hedge against Nvidia's supply and pricing.

### 5. EU AI Act starts to bite: OpenAI to watermark EU text output
**Source**: Enterprise Times / AI Policy Tracker | **Category**: Policy

OpenAI says it will add invisible watermarks to text generated by ChatGPT and Codex in the EU. This is one of the first concrete vendor changes forced by the AI Act's transparency rules. Separately, Italy published its AI Act implementing decree (Legislative Decree no. 179) on October 8.

### 6. California closes its signing season with a dozen more AI laws
**Source**: Transparency Coalition / Origin Brief | **Category**: Policy

Governor Newsom ended his bill-signing period by signing about a dozen more AI-related laws, reportedly including the No Robo Bosses Act (SB 947), which covers automated decisions in employment. At the federal level, the US relied on a voluntary industry accord, while Congress held its first sustained hearing on AI agent liability. California is acting as the de facto US regulator.

### 7. Anthropic says new measures cut distillation attacks by 90–99%
**Source**: AI Weekly | **Category**: Research

Anthropic reports that new countermeasures have reduced data harvesting through distillation attacks, meaning attempts to copy a model's capabilities by mass-querying it, by roughly 90–99%. Protecting a model from extraction by competitors is now an engineering field in its own right. Separately, Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7.

### 8. AI-enabled bank hacks in South Korea; Character.AI reportedly files Chapter 11
**Source**: AI Weekly / Innovation in the News | **Category**: Policy

South Korea's president warned that AI was used to breach seven banks, exposing 68,000 people. Separately, Character.AI has reportedly filed for Chapter 11 and agreed to sell some assets to Disney for $20M. That report comes from a single aggregator and is unconfirmed.
