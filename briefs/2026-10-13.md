# AI News Brief — 2026-10-13

## Overview
Compute and money are the main story today: Amazon is circling a $7B infrastructure deal, Huawei is closing in on Nvidia in China, and publishers and EU regulators keep tightening the rules on how model output may be produced and labelled.

## Top Stories

### 1. Amazon in talks to acquire Decart for about $7 billion
**Source**: The Wall Street Journal (via Reuters, GeekWire) | **Category**: Industry

The WSJ reported on Oct 11 that Amazon is negotiating to buy Decart, a three-year-old startup best known for the Decart Optimization Stack, which runs models across Nvidia, Google TPU and Amazon Trainium silicon. Decart also builds the Lucy real-time video model and the Oasis world model. It was valued at about $4B in May, and Anthropic reportedly walked away from a roughly $6B deal in August after due diligence. If Amazon closes, it gets cross-chip portability software that would make Trainium easier to adopt. Neither company has commented, and nothing is signed.

### 2. Nvidia's China data-center share falls to 34% as Huawei closes the gap
**Source**: Reuters (citing IDC) | **Category**: Industry

IDC data published Oct 12–13 shows Nvidia shipped 678,000 AI accelerators in China in H1 2026, down 44% year over year. Huawei shipped about 572,000 units for roughly 28% of the market. This is the clearest measure yet of how fast US export controls are turning China's AI build-out into a domestic-silicon market.

### 3. Meta debuts "Muse Wonder," a paywalled agentic model
**Source**: CNBC | **Category**: Models

CNBC reported on Oct 12 that Meta has released Muse Wonder, a new agentic model inside the Muse app, available only on its $20 and $100 monthly plans. Muse launched in September on the Muse Spark model as a personal agent that browses, fills in forms and makes purchases. Putting the newer model behind a paywall tests whether consumers will pay for agent capability rather than only for usage caps. *Only CNBC reports this so far; I couldn't find independent coverage of the model's name or specs.*

### 4. OpenAI adds invisible text watermarks to ChatGPT and Codex in the EU
**Source**: Gizmodo, Computing, Interesting Engineering | **Category**: Policy

OpenAI is rolling out "textGrain," a statistical watermark that nudges word choice, to eligible EU users of ChatGPT and Codex on every plan. The aim is to comply with Article 50 of the AI Act, which carries fines of up to €15M or 3% of global turnover. In OpenAI's own tests, swapping 10% of words for synonyms cut detection on a 400-token passage from about 92% to 66%, and only approved researchers get the detector. Coverage calls it the first visible product change forced by the Act's transparency rules. Anthropic began watermarking Claude output in August.

### 5. USA Today Co. sues OpenAI for more than $250 million
**Source**: MLex, TheDesk, Windows Report | **Category**: Policy

The suit was filed Oct 8 in the Southern District of New York (No. 1:26-cv-08892) and covers articles from 19 USA Today Co. publications. It alleges unlicensed training use, and it also claims ChatGPT summaries replace visits to the papers' sites. The complaint quotes ChatGPT head Nick Turley on summaries reducing traffic. It adds to a growing set of publisher cases: the Seattle Times and Newsday sued OpenAI and Microsoft on Sept 4 and asked the court to order the destruction of models trained on their work.

### 6. Italy publishes its AI Act implementing decree
**Source**: Enterprise Times, AI Policy Tracker | **Category**: Policy

Legislative Decree no. 179 of 7 October 2026 appeared in the Gazzetta Ufficiale on Oct 8. It brings Italian law into line with the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689). Italy joins the member states that have national enforcement plumbing in place before the Act's high-risk obligations take effect.

### 7. Sakana AI's MASS: one 27B model as solver, judge and optimizer
**Source**: AlphaSignal, Runtime Wire | **Category**: Research

Sakana AI and UC Berkeley presented Multi-Agent Self-Supervision (MASS). A single open-weights model (Qwen3.6-27B) plays every role in its own training loop: it solves tasks, rewrites the agent workflow, judges the results and fine-tunes on the trajectories it prefers. Two cycles gave 1.2–1.6× better score per output token across four research benchmarks. Ablations show the workflow optimizer, not the evaluator, is what limits the rate of self-improvement. The results are early: only two cycles were run and transfer to other tasks was uneven.

### 8. SK Networks increases its stake in Korean model maker Upstage
**Source**: Seoul Economic Daily | **Category**: Industry

SK Networks disclosed on Oct 13 that it will invest ₩20B (about $14M) in Upstage's Series C-2, raising its expected stake to 11.5%. The share purchase is scheduled for Nov 30. Upstage is one of the few independent Korean foundation-model companies, and Korean conglomerates are backing it as their national sovereign-AI bet.
