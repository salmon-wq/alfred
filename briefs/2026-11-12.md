# AI News Brief — 2026-11-12

## Overview
Frontier labs are shipping gated, cyber-capable models while Congress, states, and a fresh security breach test how much oversight the industry will accept.

## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2
**Source**: 9to5Mac / SD Times / The New Stack | **Category**: Models

Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9 as its most capable generally available model, alongside Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model restricted to vetted organizations through its Cyber and Life Sciences Verification Programs. Anthropic reports outputs up to 30% faster than 5.1 and fewer unnecessary refusals. The benchmark and pricing figures come from Anthropic and press coverage and have not been checked independently. Splitting one model into a public tier and a vetted tier is becoming the standard way labs release dual-use capability.

### 2. House Democrats' deadline for AI incident disclosures arrives
**Source**: The Hill / MLex / FedScoop | **Category**: Policy

Yesterday (November 11) was the deadline in October 28 letters from three senior House Democrats asking Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and a fifth developer to list past safety incidents. The letters also ask the companies to report new incidents within 48 hours, or 24 hours when critical infrastructure is involved. Compliance is voluntary. If Democrats take control after the midterms, as some outlets report but we have not confirmed, these requests could turn into formal oversight.

### 3. Fireworks AI expands breach disclosure: secrets, datasets, and partial model weights
**Source**: Unite.ai / ShiftDelete.Net | **Category**: Industry

Fireworks AI expanded its disclosure of an October security incident on November 9. Attackers came in through a third-party cloud provider and reached account and billing data. For a limited set of customers they also reached secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights. This contradicts the company's earlier statement that only evaluation-job environment variables were exposed. The incident shows how a single inference vendor can become a supply-chain risk for the many AI products built on it.

### 4. OpenAI closes $11B first tranche at a $1.25T valuation
**Source**: Bloomberg (via 24/7 Wall St., NewsBytes) | **Category**: Industry

OpenAI reportedly took in about $11 billion as a first close at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below the roughly $1.4 trillion it pitched to investors in October. The rest of the round is expected within weeks. The valuation is up from $852 billion in March, but it fell short of the target, and that shortfall is the first sign that investors' appetite at the top of the market has limits.

### 5. Google's Gemini 4 Carbon widens access at introductory pricing
**Source**: Google Blog / Constellation Research / 9to5Google | **Category**: Models

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28 as its strongest model, with headline gains in cybersecurity and long-horizon tasks. Access is staged, starting with cyber defenders and expanding through the Fairwind Program. Some aggregators report broader paid API availability this week, which we have not confirmed. The introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, reportedly doubling to $4 and $20 later. Google has not published a technical report.

### 6. Salesforce signs deal to buy voice-AI company Cresta
**Source**: The Next Web / CX Today / CMSWire | **Category**: Industry

Salesforce signed a definitive agreement on November 2 to acquire Cresta, whose voice agents handle customer calls for Marriott, Comcast and Alaska Airlines. The Information reported the price at $2.7 billion. The deal follows Salesforce's $5.66 billion Braze purchase and gives Agentforce a voice-first service agent alongside its text agent. Whether Salesforce can turn its run of acquisitions into one coherent product remains an open question.

## Key Themes
- **Gated frontier releases**: Anthropic (Mythos 5.2) and Google (Gemini 4 Carbon) both put their most cyber-capable models behind vetted access, so safety policy now shapes how products are distributed.
- **Incident reporting is converging**: congressional 48-hour requests, New York's RAISE Act (72 hours, effective January 1, 2027) and the EU AI Act point toward mandatory disclosure.
- **Infrastructure fragility**: the Fireworks breach shows that the security of a shared inference provider is now part of every downstream product's attack surface.
- **Capital is still flowing but getting more selective**: OpenAI's round came in below target, while strategic buyers such as Salesforce keep buying.
