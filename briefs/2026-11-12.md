# AI News Brief — 2026-11-12

## Overview
Anthropic's record IPO moves into its final week. Meanwhile frontier labs keep their strongest cyber-capable models behind vetting programs, a supply-chain breach at an inference provider shows how exposed the AI stack is, and a new Democratic Congress gets ready to legislate.

## Top Stories

### 1. Anthropic opens IPO roadshow at $70–$78 per share
**Source**: CNBC, Yahoo Finance, RTTNews | **Category**: Industry

Anthropic launched its roadshow on November 10. It is offering 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70–$78 each and will list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as `ANT`. CNBC reports pricing is planned for November 18, with trading around November 19, and that the book is already covered across the range. At the top of the range the full offering would raise about $64 billion, the largest IPO on record and the first public-market test of a frontier lab's valuation.

### 2. Claude Fable 5.2 ships, with an ungated twin, Mythos 5.2, for vetted organizations
**Source**: SD Times, The New Stack, 9to5Mac | **Category**: Models

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9. The company says it generates output up to 30% faster than 5.1 and blocks about 55% fewer legitimate requests. Claude Mythos 5.2 is the same model without the safeguards on cybersecurity and research-level biology work. It is offered only through Anthropic's Cyber Verification and Life Sciences Verification programs. The benchmark and pricing figures come from Anthropic, and outlets report them inconsistently.

### 3. Fireworks AI widens breach disclosure: customer datasets and model weights accessed
**Source**: Fireworks AI security bulletin, ai-tldr.dev | **Category**: Industry

On November 9, Fireworks AI expanded its account of an October intrusion. Attackers came in through a third-party cloud provider hosting its R&D machines. They reached account and billing data and, for a limited set of customers, stored secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights. The first bulletin had said only evaluation-job secrets were exposed. Fireworks has paused self-serve dataset uploads and downloads while it investigates. For any team that sends fine-tuning data to a hosted provider, this is a clear supply-chain lesson.

### 4. OpenAI acquires AI travel agent Odessia, following an $11B raise at $1.25T
**Source**: Bloomberg (via AI Funding Tracker), Crypto Briefing | **Category**: Industry

On November 10, OpenAI bought Odessia, a Sequoia-backed AI travel concierge, hired its founder Francis Davidson (formerly a co-founder of Sonder) and shut the product down. It is an acqui-hire into OpenAI's consumer-agent push. Bloomberg reported on November 6 that OpenAI has raised $11 billion so far in a round valuing it at $1.25 trillion, so both leading labs are raising very large sums in the same month.

### 5. Google limits Gemini 4 Carbon to cyber defenders; Argon's introductory pricing ends November 30
**Source**: Khaleej Times, MindStudio, eesel.ai | **Category**: Models

Gemini 4 Carbon, Google's new frontier model with a cybersecurity focus, has no public API. Access is limited to defenders in Google's Fairwind program, at a reported $2/M input and $10/M output tokens. Gemini 4 Argon, the generally available model, keeps that introductory rate until November 30, when it doubles to $4/$20. Teams running Argon at scale should plan for the higher rate. Carbon's own pricing schedule has not been confirmed.

### 6. A Democratic Congress prepares an AI agenda; Scott Wiener wins Pelosi's seat
**Source**: MIT Technology Review, Reason, Reuters | **Category**: Policy

After the midterms, Rep. Ted Lieu said a Democrat-led Congress will call AI companies to hearings and push for "reasonable guardrails." Scott Wiener, who wrote California's frontier-AI safety legislation, won the race for Nancy Pelosi's San Francisco seat. Reuters reports draft proposals on data-centre electricity costs, independent testing of advanced models, and AI-company liability for harm. Federal preemption of state laws, the White House's priority, now looks less likely.

### 7. Australia moves toward a frontier-AI regulator with penalty powers
**Source**: The Conversation, White & Case, Baker McKenzie | **Category**: Policy

The Conversation reports that the Albanese government has announced a new AI regulator able to impose significant penalties on frontier developers. It is pairing the regulator with six mandatory safety guardrails and is weighing criminal-law changes for serious misconduct. Earlier statements said the regime would be modelled on banking and aviation regulation. The move follows the government's criticism of OpenAI's late notice of an agent incident on a government system. The exact announcement date and penalty levels could not be confirmed from primary sources.
